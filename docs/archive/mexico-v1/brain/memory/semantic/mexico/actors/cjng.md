## Identity

The Jalisco New Generation Cartel (Cártel de Jalisco Nueva Generación, CJNG) is a Mexican criminal organization founded in 2009 as a splinter of the Sinaloa Cartel. Under the leadership of Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes "El Mencho" (2011-2026), it grew into Mexico's most powerful and violent cartel, operating in 27 of 32 states and maintaining international drug trafficking networks spanning five continents. The CJNG is distinguished by its paramilitary capabilities—including use of drones, armored vehicles, and heavy weapons—and its diversified revenue portfolio: synthetic drugs (fentanyl, methamphetamine), heroin, cocaine, extortion, fuel theft, mining, avocado production, and migrant smuggling. The organization is structured as a cellular network of semi-autonomous "plazas" (territorial cells) coordinated by a central leadership, a model that has proven resilient to leadership decapitation.

## Recent Developments

**February 22, 2026:** El Mencho killed during a Mexican Army operation in Tapalpa, Jalisco. Multiple independent confirmations from Proceso and ACLED (A2/B2, May 12). The operation involved 200+ special forces soldiers and helicopter support. El Mencho was killed in a firefight; his body was positively identified through fingerprints and DNA.

**March 18, 2026:** The Wall Street Journal (A1) reported that Juan Carlos Valencia González "El Pelón" / "El 03" had assumed leadership of CJNG. Valencia González is El Mencho's stepson and a US citizen, born in California. El País (A2) confirmed on April 6.

**April 27, 2026:** Audías Flores Silva "El Jardinero" captured by the Mexican Navy in Nayarit. Flores Silva was a senior CJNG lieutenant and potential successor. ACLED analyst Sandra Pellegrini (A2/B2, May 12) described his capture as a "tactical blow" with localized effects, not organizational. The Washington Times (B2, May 14) reported that Flores Silva is being held in a maximum-security prison in Nayarit pending potential extradition to the US on drug trafficking charges.

**May 3, 2026:** A CJNG-aligned cell attacked a fuel pipeline in Salamanca, Guanajuato, stealing an estimated 50,000 liters of gasoline. The attack involved 30 armed men who overwhelmed security personnel. [Milenio (B2), May 4]

**May 8, 2026:** CJNG operatives assassinated a municipal police commander in Tonalá, Jalisco, in retaliation for a raid that seized 200 kg of methamphetamine. The commander was shot 15 times outside his home. [Reforma (B2), May 9]

**May 12, 2026:** ACLED published a comprehensive assessment concluding CJNG maintains intact military power and criminal markets. "No evidence of fracture." Characterizes the current period as "tactical containment" and organizational adaptation rather than decline.

**May 14, 2026:** The Washington Times (B2) reported that US federal prosecutors are "eyeing" Valencia González for indictment, leveraging his US citizenship for expedited prosecution. No formal charges have been filed.

**May 16, 2026:** A CJNG cell in Colima state attacked a federal police convoy with drones carrying explosives, wounding three officers. This marks the first documented use of drone-based IEDs by CJNG since El Mencho's death. [Proceso (B2), May 16]

## Softeners

1. **Valencia González US citizenship vulnerability:** If indicted in the US, CJNG could face a second leadership crisis within 12 months. His US citizenship makes prosecution easier and extradition more likely. The Washington Times (B2, May 14) reports prosecutors are "eyeing" him. A successful US prosecution would decapitate CJNG twice in two years.

2. **El Jardinero extradition risk:** Flores Silva's capture and potential extradition could provide US prosecutors with high-value intelligence on CJNG financial networks, operational structures, and international connections. His cooperation could trigger cascading arrests.

3. **Internal succession tensions:** While no evidence of hostile competition exists, the transition from El Mencho to his stepson may not be universally accepted. The CJNG's cellular structure means regional plaza bosses could assert autonomy if Valencia González proves weak or compromised.

4. **Sinaloa Cartel internal war resolution:** The CDS internal war between Los Chapitos and El Mayo factions could eventually resolve, allowing CDS to refocus on CJNG competition. A unified CDS would be a more formidable adversary.

5. **US political pressure:** The Biden administration (or successor) could escalate pressure on Mexico to deliver Valencia González, potentially straining bilateral relations and creating operational disruptions.

## Tighteners

1. **Institutional resilience confirmed:** If the current non-fracture pattern holds through Q3 2026, the framework assumption must update: CJNG is institutionally resilient to decapitation. This would represent a fundamental shift in understanding of Mexican cartel dynamics.

2. **Sinaloa Cartel internal weakness:** The CDS internal war may permanently weaken their position against CJNG in contested territories (Baja California, Sonora, Ciudad Juárez, Zacatecas, Chiapas). CJNG could consolidate gains.

3. **Drone capability expansion:** The May 16 drone-based IED attack in Colima suggests CJNG is maintaining or expanding its technological edge. If drone attacks become routine, CJNG could achieve tactical superiority in certain operational environments.

4. **Revenue stream continuity:** Chris Dalby/Dyami Security Intelligence (B2) notes: "Same thing that happens in any multinational when the head is lost — the economic structure keeps operating." Revenue from the Mencho-era portfolio (drugs, extortion, fuel contraband) continues unchanged, funding sustained operations.

5. **Recruitment pipeline intact:** No evidence of recruitment slowdown. CJNG's reputation for violence and payment reliability continues to attract recruits, particularly in Jalisco, Michoacán, and Colima.

6. **International network stability:** CJNG's international trafficking networks (US, Europe, Asia, Australia) remain operational. No reports of supply chain disruptions post-Mencho.

## Forward Assessment

**Judgment 1:** CJNG will maintain organizational cohesion through Q3 2026, with no significant fracture or internal rebellion. The cellular structure and diversified revenue portfolio provide institutional resilience that compensates for leadership decapitation.
- **Sherman Kent band:** 70-85% probability (likely)
- **Rationale:** Three months of post-Mencho stability, no evidence of internal competition, intact revenue streams, and the cellular model's inherent redundancy all support this judgment. The primary risk is Valencia González's US citizenship triggering prosecution.

**Judgment 2:** Violence levels in CJNG-controlled territories will remain stable or decrease slightly through Q3 2026, as the organization prioritizes internal consolidation over territorial expansion.
- **Sherman Kent band:** 60-75% probability (likely)
- **Rationale:** ACLED data shows decreasing violence in Jalisco (tactical containment). The organization is adapting rather than fighting. However, the May 16 drone attack and May 3 fuel theft suggest operational activity continues. The risk is that consolidation efforts could trigger localized violence if plaza bosses resist central control.

**Judgment 3:** Valencia González will face US indictment within 12 months, creating a second leadership crisis. However, CJNG will survive this crisis as well, though with potential operational disruption.
- **Sherman Kent band:** 55-70% probability (likely)
- **Rationale:** His US citizenship makes him a high-value target for US prosecutors. The Washington Times (B2, May 14) confirms prosecutors are "eyeing" him. Historical patterns (e.g., El Chapo's extradition) suggest US-Mexico cooperation on high-value targets. However, the timing depends on evidence gathering and political considerations.
