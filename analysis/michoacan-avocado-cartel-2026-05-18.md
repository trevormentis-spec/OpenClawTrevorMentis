# Michoacán Avocado × Cartel Territorial Control — Analytical Brief

## BLUF
CJNG controls an estimated 80% of Michoacán territory (Milenio/Mexico Daily Post, Feb 2026) and skims ~$770M annually from the avocado industry (Times Now, Feb 2026, single-source estimate), but the assassination of leader El Mencho on February 22, 2026 has created a succession crisis that threatens to destabilize the extortion apparatus just as the 2026 harvest season peaks.

## Industry Overview

**Production Scale:** Michoacán produces 92% of Mexico's avocados across 106,000 hectares (Wikipedia/WikipedIAN, March 2026). This represents near-total dominance of Mexico's avocado output, making the state the de facto global supplier for US consumption.

**Export Value:** US-bound avocado exports from Michoacán are valued at approximately $2.9 billion annually (Times Now, Feb 2026, citing food export data). This figure represents the legal export value; the cartel-extracted portion adds a significant illicit overlay.

**Geographic Concentration:** 31 municipalities are PGI-certified for avocado production (FreshFruitPortal, May 11, 2026). Key producing municipalities include Uruapan, Tancítaro, Tacámbaro, Zitácuaro, and Buenavista (same source). These five municipalities form the core of the avocado economy, though production extends across the certified zone.

**GAP: No municipality-level avocado production share data exists in open sources.** The percentage of total exports originating from Uruapan versus Tancítaro, for example, is unknown. This gap prevents precise targeting of cartel revenue extraction by location.

## Cartel Landscape

### CJNG (Cártel Jalisco Nueva Generación)
- **Territory:** Controls an estimated 80% of Michoacán territory (Milenio/Mexico Daily Post, Feb 2026, citing DEA and security sources). This includes the primary avocado-producing corridor.
- **Revenue Model (Avocado-Specific):** The ~$770M annual skim (Times Now, Feb 2026, single-source estimate from US/DEA-adjacent reporting) is believed to derive from "piso" (ground tax) on producers, packing house fees, and transportation route extortion. **GAP: No breakdown exists of how this $770M is distributed across piso tax, packing house control, or transport fees.**
- **Current Conflict Posture:** Active conflict with Viagras/Nueva Familia Michoacana in Uruapan area (OCCRP, El Financiero, Mar 2026). CJNG massacred 19 Viagras members in Uruapan area in 2025 (START.umd.edu). Recent gunfight killed 9 in Michoacán (OCCRP, date unspecified). Succession following El Mencho's death (Feb 22, 2026) under El Pelón is ongoing and likely contested.

### Viagras / Nueva Familia Michoacana (NFM)
- **Territory:** Operating primarily in Uruapan and surrounding areas, contesting CJNG control. **GAP: No confirmed percentage of territory controlled by Viagras/NFM in avocado zones.**
- **Revenue Model (Avocado-Specific):** Likely similar piso tax model to CJNG, but on a smaller scale. **GAP: No revenue estimates exist for Viagras/NFM avocado extortion.**
- **Current Conflict Posture:** Defensive against CJNG after 2025 massacre. Active combat in Uruapan (OCCRP, El Financiero, Mar 2026).

### Other Groups
- At least 20 armed groups operate in Michoacán (Milenio/DEA sources, Feb 2026). **GAP: No mapping exists of which groups control which avocado-producing municipalities beyond the CJNG/Viagras binary.**

## Municipal-Level Analysis

**Known Data vs. Known Gaps:** Municipality-level data IS available for which municipalities are PGI-certified avocado producers (FreshFruitPortal, May 11, 2026). Municipality-level data IS NOT available for cartel control mapping, production share, or extortion revenue at the municipal level.

## Observable Indicators

Trackable signals for subscribers:

1. **Violence spikes in Uruapan:** Monitor OCCRP, El Financiero, and local Michoacán press for CJNG-Viagras clashes. Increased violence suggests succession-related instability in CJNG's avocado extortion apparatus.

2. **Avocado export volume deviations:** Monitor US import data (USDA/APHIS) for month-over-month declines from Michoacán. A 10%+ drop coinciding with violence spikes would indicate supply chain disruption.

3. **Packing house closures:** Track reports of packing house closures or ownership changes in Uruapan and Tancítaro via FreshFruitPortal and industry trade press. This signals cartel renegotiation of extortion terms.

4. **El Pelón consolidation signals:** Monitor CJNG internal communications (if available via DEA/security sources) for evidence of successful or contested succession. Failure to consolidate would likely increase violence in avocado zones.

5. **New group emergence:** With 20 armed groups already operating (Milenio/DEA, Feb 2026), monitor for new entrants into avocado-producing municipalities, particularly in Tacámbaro, Zitácuaro, and Buenavista where current control is unknown.

## Data Gaps (explicit)

Every gap where municipal-level quantification is unavailable but would change the assessment if filled:

1. **GAP: Municipality-level avocado production share.** Without knowing what percentage of exports come from Uruapan vs. Tancítaro vs. other municipalities, it is impossible to prioritize which conflict zones pose the greatest supply chain risk.

2. **GAP: Municipality-level cartel control mapping.** The 80% CJNG control figure (Milenio/Mexico Daily Post, Feb 2026) is state-level. Which specific municipalities are CJNG-controlled, which are contested, and which are held by other groups is unknown.

3. **GAP: Per-municipality extortion revenue estimates.** The $770M figure (Times Now, Feb 2026, single-source estimate) is state-level. Allocation across municipalities would reveal which cartel groups control the most valuable avocado territory.

4. **GAP: CJNG post-Mencho impact on Michoacán avocado operations.** El Mencho's death (Feb 22, 2026) and El Pelón's succession may have disrupted or reorganized avocado extortion networks. No assessment exists.

5. **GAP: INEGI municipal export data for avocado.** INEGI (Mexico's national statistics agency) has not released municipal-level avocado export data that would enable independent verification of production shares.

6. **GAP: Avocado packing house control by cartel.** Which cartels control which packing houses—the critical chokepoint between grower and exporter—is unknown. This is likely where the majority of extortion occurs.

7. **GAP: Transportation route extortion data specific to avocado supply chain.** Cartels likely extract fees at highway checkpoints on routes from producing municipalities to processing/export hubs. No data exists on which routes are controlled by which groups.

8. **GAP: Viagras/NFM territorial control percentage.** No estimate exists for the share of avocado-producing territory controlled by Viagras/NFM, making it impossible to assess their revenue base.

9. **GAP: Tacámbaro, Zitácuaro, and Buenavista cartel dynamics.** These three PGI-certified municipalities have no open-source reporting on current cartel presence, control, or conflict status.

## Calibration

**Sherman Kent Confidence Band: Moderate-Low**

**Directional Thesis:** CJNG controls the majority of Michoacán's avocado export industry and extracts significant revenue through extortion, but the post-Mencho succession creates vulnerability that Viagras/NFM may exploit, potentially disrupting supply chains.

**Confidence Assessment:**
- **High confidence:** Michoacán produces 92% of Mexico's avocados (Wikipedia/WikipedIAN, March 2026). CJNG controls ~80% of Michoacán territory (Milenio/Mexico Daily Post, Feb 2026, DEA-sourced). Active conflict exists in Uruapan (OCCRP, El Financiero, Mar 2026).
- **Moderate confidence:** The $770M annual skim figure (Times Now, Feb 2026, single-source estimate) is plausible but unverified. The 20 armed groups figure (Milenio/DEA, Feb 2026) is credible but lacks granularity.
- **Low confidence:** Any assessment of post-Mencho impact on avocado operations, municipality-level control, or packing house dynamics. These are entirely gap-driven.

**Key Uncertainty Drivers:**
1. The $770M figure is a single-source estimate from DEA-adjacent reporting—no independent verification exists.
2. The 80% CJNG territorial control figure is state-level and may not reflect avocado-zone control specifically.
3. Post-Mencho succession dynamics are unfolding in real-time with no open-source assessment available.
4. Five of the six key avocado municipalities have no confirmed cartel control data.

**If the following gaps were filled, confidence would increase to Moderate-High:**
- Municipality-level production shares
- Municipality-level cartel control mapping
- Post-Mencho impact assessment on avocado extortion networks
- Packing house control data

**Bottom Line:** The directional thesis is likely correct—CJNG dominates Michoacán avocado extortion—but the magnitude ($770M), the geographic distribution, and the post-Mencho trajectory remain unverified. Subscribers should treat the $770M figure as a plausible upper bound rather than a confirmed estimate.
