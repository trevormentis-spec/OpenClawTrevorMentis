## [Conflict (Stephen Roach)] Trump’s Vanity Summit
- url: https://stephenroach.substack.com/p/trumps-vanity-summit
- date: Fri, 25 Sep 2026 22:01:54 GMT

Long on ceremony a but short on substance, the long-awaited Trump-Xi summit was a disappointment. I wouldn't call it a major disappointment, since pre-summit expectations were generally low. But on the big issues of trade, AI, Taiwan, and addressing two serious wars, there was little or no progress. That is what disappointment looks like.
 On trade, a two-month extension of the truce &#8212; negotiated nearly a year ago in Busan, South Korea &#8212; was surprisingly short. Pre-summit chatter on the extension had been in the range of six to twelve months. Moreover, expectations had focused on another round of Chinese purchases of US exports. Normally those negotiations are completed before a summit even begins. Not so this time. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has hinted we may see something on incremental purchases in a few days. But don't hold your breath for successful implementation, even if that happens. The Phase I trade deal failed miserably on that count and recent reports of farm goods purchases are tracking well below earlier Chinese promises. The bottom line on trade: Limited truce extension implies that tariff uncertainty remains high.
 


 
 On AI, the two superpowers have done the minimum. They committed to ongoing discussions, rather than a formally structured dialog. And they dangled the possibility of an unspecified AI communications "hot line ." The likelihood of a major breakthrough on AI safety, as a result, is lower than many had hoped for &#8212; especially in the aftermath of the recent Hugging Face hacking incident and reports of other AI intrusions. However, in light of President Trump's recent "hands-off" statement on US intervention in domestic AI security, this week's summit outcome on AI cannot be considered a major disappointment.
 On Taiwan, Xi gets the nod from the standpoint of power projection. He continued to press the advantage he gained from Trump last May, when on the plane ride back home from the Beijing summit, the US president described the pending $14 billion arms sale to Taiwan as a good "negotiating chip." The Chinese side considered this as a major concession from America's previous unwavering commitment to delivering on defensive arms packages that had already been approved by the Congress. In his opening speech to this week's summit, Xi was very clear in stressing that the US should "adhere to the correct position of opposing Taiwan independence." This would be a stunning backtracking from the "strategic ambiguity" that has long shaped US strategy toward Taiwanese sovereignty.
 Finally, there appears to have been no attention addressed to China's key role in supporting the serious wars in Ukraine and Iran. As the buyer of 90% of Iran's oil exports and about 40% of the oil sent abroad by Russia, China has played a major role in funding the aggressive military operations of both nations. Passage of a recent bill on secondary sanctions now gives the US president broad authority to address this activity through highly punitive tariffs. Don't count on it. There appears to have been no mention of this "hot potato" at yesterday's summit.
 By doing the minimum on the big issues, both in terms of staff preparation and in short bilateral chats, Donald Trump was painfully transparent on what mattered most to him in this summit. He went beyond any semblance of normal protocol in declaring heartfelt admiration for his " truly great " friend. Add to that, a multiplicity of red carpets that would even put China to shame, the glitzy State dinner with all the tech bros, and his personal tour of Xi to show him all the new vanity projects in the White House &#8212; the granite helipad, the ballroom construction project, and a walkthrough of his rogues' gallery of annotated presidential portraits &#8212; there can be little doubt of how the Trump-Xi Summit of September 24, 2026, summit will be remembered. It was America's first vanity summit.
