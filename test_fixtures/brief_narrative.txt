## SECTION 1: ASIA-PACIFIC SECURITY — TAIWAN STRAIT

People's Liberation Army (PLA) activity around Taiwan has sustained elevated tempo through the final quarter of 2024 and into 2025, with the PLA Eastern Theater Command conducting the "Joint Sword-2024B" exercises in October 2024 — a multi-axis envelopment drill involving the Shandong carrier strike group, coast guard formations, and approximately 153 aircraft tracked in a single 25-hour window, a record daily incursion across the Taiwan Strait median line. December 2024 saw a further unannounced surge featuring roughly 90 PLA Navy and China Coast Guard hulls dispersed across the First Island Chain, which Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense characterized as the largest maritime mobilization in nearly three decades. Reporting indicates J-20 stealth fighters have been forward-deployed to Anshan and Wuyishan airbases, placing fifth-generation assets within unrefueled combat radius of Taipei and southern Japanese airspace. The deployment pattern suggests institutionalization rather than episodic signaling.

The US response has combined visible deterrence with measured restraint. The Nimitz-class USS Carl Vinson conducted dual-carrier operations with the Ronald Reagan replacement assets in the Philippine Sea, while the December 2024 $571 million Presidential Drawdown Authority package to Taiwan accelerated F-16V munitions and HIMARS-compatible stocks. Typhon mid-range missile system deployment to the Philippines, retained beyond originally announced timelines, signals a hardening US regional posture. AUKUS Pillar II coordination on undersea sensors has likewise quickened.

**Judgment 1:** It is *likely* (60–70%) that PLA exercise tempo through mid-2025 will continue escalating in scope and complexity, normalizing operations east of the median line and compressing Taiwan's strategic warning timeline below 48 hours.

**Judgment 2:** A deliberate invasion in 2025 remains *unlikely* (15–25%); however, the *probability of inadvertent escalation* from intercept incidents — particularly involving J-20 and US/Japanese aircraft over disputed ADIZ overlap — has risen to *roughly even chances*, driven by tighter geometries and degraded crisis communication channels following the 2023 mil-to-mil rupture.

## SECTION 2: MIDDLE EAST & IRAN — NUCLEAR PROGRAM

Iran's nuclear program has crossed thresholds that render the 2015 JCPOA framework substantively obsolete. The IAEA's November 2024 Board of Governors report assessed Iranian stockpiles of uranium enriched to 60% U-235 at approximately 182 kilograms — sufficient, if further enriched to weapons-grade, for three to four nuclear devices. Configuration changes at the Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant (FFEP), where Iran reconfigured IR-6 centrifuge cascades to enable rapid 60% production, were notified in December 2024 following Iranian retaliation for the November IAEA censure resolution co-sponsored by the E3 and United States. The Natanz Pilot Fuel Enrichment Plant has shown analogous reconfiguration. Inspector access at undeclared sites — Turquzabad, Varamin, and Marivan — remains unresolved, and the September 2023 designation removal of senior inspectors continues to degrade verification fidelity.

Diplomatic tracks remain in stasis. Indirect Omani-mediated US-Iran contacts in late 2024 produced no procedural breakthrough, and Tehran has signaled that the incoming US administration's "maximum pressure" reimposition would foreclose negotiations. Israeli strikes in October 2024 against Iranian air defenses — degrading S-300 batteries protecting Natanz and Parchin — have meaningfully altered the strike-feasibility calculus for any prospective campaign against nuclear infrastructure. Iranian leadership has publicly debated revising its declaratory nuclear doctrine, with statements from Kamal Kharrazi suggesting reconsideration of the supreme leader's 2003 fatwa.

**Judgment 1:** It is *highly likely* (80–90%) that Iran's breakout time to one significant quantity of weapons-grade uranium now stands at one to two weeks, though weaponization — warhead integration and delivery — would *probably* (55–70%) require an additional 12–18 months absent foreign assistance.

**Judgment 2:** An Israeli or US-Israeli kinetic strike on Fordow within the next 6–9 months is a *serious possibility* (35–45%), conditional on detection of weaponization-related activity at Parchin or further Iranian doctrinal shifts.

## SECTION 3: NATO EASTERN FLANK

NATO's eastern flank has experienced a substantive deterioration in baseline security through late 2024. Repeated drone incursions into Romanian, Latvian, and Polish airspace — most notably the November 2024 Shahed-136 debris recoveries in Latvia and the recurring incursions over Romania's Tulcea County — have prompted activation of Article 4 consultations and the standing up of integrated air defense surge cells. Poland reinforced its eastern frontier with the "Eastern Shield" program, a €2.3 billion fortification effort along the Belarusian and Kaliningrad borders incorporating anti-drone meshes, hardened command nodes, and pre-positioned engineering obstacles. Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania have jointly committed to a Baltic Defense Line of approximately 600 bunkers, with construction accelerating after the December 2024 Estlink-2 submarine cable severance — assessed by Finnish authorities as deliberate Russian shadow-fleet sabotage involving the tanker Eagle S.

NATO multinational battlegroups in the Baltic states and Poland have transitioned from battalion to brigade-equivalent posture, with the German-led Lithuania brigade reaching initial operating capability through 2025 and reaching full strength by 2027. US V Corps forward command in Poznań retains rotational armored brigade combat teams, while UK and French commitments to the Steadfast Defender 2024 exercise framework — the largest NATO exercise since 1988, involving 90,000 personnel — have validated transatlantic reinforcement timelines.

**Judgment 1:** It is *likely* (60–75%) that Russian hybrid activity — cable cutting, GPS jamming across the Baltic basin, and arson operations attributed to GRU Unit 29155 — will intensify through 2025 as a substitute for conventional escalation, given Russian ground force degradation in Ukraine.

**Judgment 2:** A deliberate Russian conventional incursion against a NATO member is *unlikely* (10–20%) within the next 12 months; however, a miscalculation scenario involving drone overflight engagement is a *serious possibility* (30–40%), particularly over Romania.

## SECTION 4: GLOBAL FINANCE & MARKETS

Global financial markets are exhibiting a layered risk-on/risk-off bifurcation reflecting geopolitical premium pricing alongside resilient US growth data. Gold, the canonical safe-haven, traded above $2,700/oz through Q4 2024 and into early 2025, with central bank purchasing — particularly from the People's Bank of China, which resumed reported acquisitions in November 2024 after a six-month pause, and from the Reserve Bank of India — providing structural support beyond speculative flows. The Swiss franc and Japanese yen, however, have decoupled from traditional haven behavior: the yen weakened past 158 against the dollar in January 2025 as the Bank of Japan's normalization remains gradualist, while US Treasury yields above 4.6% on the 10-year reflect fiscal supply concerns rather than pure flight-to-safety.

The semiconductor supply chain remains the central structural vulnerability. US Bureau of Industry and Security export controls finalized in December 2024 expanded HBM (high-bandwidth memory) restrictions and added 140 Chinese entities including key SMIC affiliates and lithography toolmakers to the Entity List. TSMC's Arizona Fab 21 began 4nm production in late 2024, but bleeding-edge 2nm and CoWoS advanced packaging remain concentrated in Taiwan — preserving the chokepoint risk discussed in Section 1. ASML's January 2025 guidance flagged China demand softening from the export control regime.

Energy markets have absorbed Middle East risk premia unevenly: Brent has traded in a $72–82 band despite Red Sea Houthi interdiction continuing into 2025, reflecting OPEC+ spare capacity and weak Chinese demand. European TTF gas prices, however, spiked above €50/MWh in January 2025 following the January 1 termination of Russian transit through Ukraine.

**Judgment 1:** It is *likely* (65–75%) that gold sustains levels above $2,600/oz through 2025, supported by central bank diversification and fiscal-dominance concerns in major economies.

**Judgment 2:** A Taiwan Strait disruption affecting TSMC operations would *almost certainly* (90%+) trigger a 15–25% global equity correction within 72 hours, with semiconductor-dependent indices (SOX, TPX) experiencing disorderly drawdowns exceeding 35%.
