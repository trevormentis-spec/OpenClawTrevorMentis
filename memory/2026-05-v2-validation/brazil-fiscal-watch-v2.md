# Brazil Fiscal Policy — H1 2026 Watch Brief

## Bottom Line Up Front

Brazil enters H1 2026 with fiscal arithmetic that no longer balances under realistic political constraints. A 2.3% primary deficit against gross debt approaching 78% of GDP, with Selic anchored at 14.75% to defend against a Real trading near 5.80, produces an interest bill consuming roughly 7-8% of GDP — a structural drain that the new fiscal framework was explicitly designed to halt but is now visibly failing to contain. Mandatory spending growth (real minimum wage indexation, BPC, healthcare floors) is mechanically crowding out the discretionary envelope, and the Lula administration's political capital for further consolidation measures is depleted heading into pre-electoral positioning for October 2026.

The base case is fiscal drift, not fiscal crisis. We assess Brazil retains market access, IMF dialogue remains constructive, and the BCB's institutional credibility under Galípolo — despite earlier market skepticism — has held the curve. However, the path of least political resistance is incremental framework dilution rather than rupture, and this is being priced into the long end of the NTN-B curve (real yields above 7.5% on 2035s signal a structural risk premium, not a tactical mispricing).

For family offices with EM exposure, the next six months present a bifurcated trade: carry remains exceptional on a hedged basis, but duration and equity beta carry asymmetric tail risk tied to the 2027 fiscal framework review and 2026 electoral signaling. Position accordingly.

## Watch Item 1: Fiscal Framework Credibility Erosion

**Heading:** Will the arcabouço fiscal survive H1 2026 intact, or will Congress legislate around it?

The new fiscal framework permits real expenditure growth of 0.6-2.5% indexed to revenue, with primary deficit targets ratcheting toward balance by 2026. Both anchors are now under visible stress. The 2025 outturn missed the central target band, requiring the government to invoke tolerance provisions, and the 2026 target (0.0% ± 0.25%) is mathematically incompatible with current revenue trajectories absent extraordinary measures. We expect at least one of three pressure releases in H1: (1) reclassification of expenditures outside the cap (precatórios precedent), (2) target band widening via PLP amendment, or (3) revenue-side one-offs (dividend extraction from Petrobras/BNDES, asset sales). Each erodes framework credibility marginally; cumulative erosion is the actual risk. Haddad's Fazenda team is fighting a rearguard action against the Casa Civil and the PT base, and the relationship with Congress under Motta and Alcolumbre is transactional, not aligned.

**Confidence:** highly_likely (80-92%) that framework dilution occurs in H1 2026 via at least one mechanism.

**Source rating:** B2 — official Fazenda communications, IMF Article IV, congressional reporting; usually reliable, probably true.

## Watch Item 2: Monetary-Fiscal Standoff and the Selic Path

**Heading:** Galípolo's credibility test — can the BCB cut into a deteriorating fiscal picture?

Selic at 14.75% reflects the BCB's response to unanchored inflation expectations (5-year breakevens above the 3% target by roughly 150bps) and FX passthrough from a Real that has lost ~15% YTD against the dollar. The market currently prices ~150bps of cuts through end-2026, but this pricing is highly conditional. If fiscal slippage materializes as we expect, the BCB faces a choice: cut aggressively to ease debt dynamics (politically demanded, institutionally corrosive) or hold to defend the inflation anchor (politically costly, credibility-positive). Galípolo, contrary to early-2025 market positioning, has demonstrated hawkish revealed preferences and an unwillingness to front-run fiscal accommodation. We expect a shallow, data-dependent easing cycle — perhaps 75-100bps in H1 — conditional on disinflation continuing and the Real stabilizing. A premature or politically-coded cut would trigger curve steepening and Real weakness that would force reversal, a scenario the BCB will work to avoid.

**Confidence:** likely (63-79%) that Selic ends H1 2026 between 13.50-14.25%, with BCB maintaining hawkish communication.

**Source rating:** B2 — COPOM minutes, BCB Inflation Report, market consensus; reliable institutional sources.

## Watch Item 3: Tax Reform Implementation and Revenue Realization

**Heading:** PEC 45 transition — fiscal upside or implementation drag?

The consumption tax reform (CBS/IBS replacing PIS/Cofins/ICMS/ISS) enters its phased implementation with 2026 as the first test year (1% test rates). The reform is structurally positive — eliminating cascading taxation, improving allocative efficiency, with Fazenda estimating GDP gains of 12-20% over 15 years — but the H1 2026 fiscal contribution will be negligible to slightly negative due to transition costs, compliance investment, and the regulatory build-out of the Comitê Gestor do IBS. The risk is twofold: implementation friction generates political backlash from sectors facing effective rate increases (services, financial intermediation), and the federative compensation mechanism creates state-level fiscal pressures that may require federal backstops. Watch for the regulamentação complementar votes in Congress, particularly the cashback mechanism and the selective tax (Imposto Seletivo) scope — both are battlegrounds where lobby pressure could weaken the reform's revenue neutrality assumption and create unbudgeted fiscal holes.

**Confidence:** chances_about_even (40-62%) that implementation proceeds without material fiscal slippage attributable to reform transition in H1 2026.

**Source rating:** C3 — sector analyses, congressional committee reports, Receita Federal guidance; fairly reliable but with implementation uncertainty.

## Strategic Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Run Carry, Hedge Tail — Asymmetric Position Sizing on BRL Local Rates

The carry trade in Brazilian local rates remains structurally attractive: ~14% nominal yields on the short end, real yields above 7.5% on the long end, against a currency that has already absorbed significant adjustment. However, the distribution of outcomes is asymmetric — the upside is realized carry plus modest curve bull-flattening if disinflation continues; the downside is a fiscal credibility event that drives the long end 200bps wider and the Real to 6.50+. We recommend positioning in the belly of the curve (2027-2029 NTN-Fs) where carry-to-volatility is optimized, paired with out-of-the-money USDBRL call options (strikes 6.20-6.50, 6-month tenor) financed by partial premium recovery from selling shorter-dated puts. Avoid the long end (2030+) until post-electoral fiscal framework clarity emerges in Q4 2026. Target net exposure: 3-5% of EM allocation, with explicit stop-loss tied to 5-year CDS breaching 250bps (currently ~200bps).

### Recommendation 2: Underweight Brazil Equity Beta; Selective Exposure to FX-Insulated Cash Generators

Brazilian equities trade at a discount to historical multiples that appears optically attractive but is justified by the cost-of-capital regime. With Selic at 14.75% and likely to remain above 13% through H1, the domestic discount rate punishes long-duration equity cash flows and compresses multiples on consumer, retail, and infrastructure names. We recommend underweighting the Ibovespa on a beta-adjusted basis and selecting individual names with three characteristics: (1) USD-linked or commodity-linked revenue (Vale, Suzano, Petrobras at appropriate entry), (2) net cash balance sheets or short-duration liabilities, (3) dividend yields above 8% with sustainable payout coverage. Avoid domestically-funded growth names, leveraged real estate, and any business model dependent on consumer credit expansion. Position size should reflect that a fiscal accident scenario produces a 25-35% drawdown in BRL terms and a 35-45% drawdown in USD terms.

### Recommendation 3: Establish Optionality on the 2026 Electoral Cycle Without Pre-Committing

The October 2026 election introduces a discontinuity that markets will increasingly price from Q2 onward. Three scenarios warrant pre-positioned optionality rather than directional bets: (a) Lula re-election with weakened Congress — base case, modest negative for risk assets; (b) center-right victory with fiscal reform mandate — meaningful re-rating positive for Brazilian assets, particularly long-duration equity and the long end of the curve; (c) fragmented outcome with governance paralysis — meaningfully negative, with fiscal framework rupture risk. We recommend building optionality through long-dated EWZ call spreads (12-month, struck 10-20% OTM) and long-dated Brazil sovereign CDS protection (7-10 year tenor where the curve is relatively flat). The cost of carrying both is modest (~80-120bps annualized on notional), but the structure pays in both tail scenarios while remaining capital-efficient in the base case. Review positioning quarterly against polling data, fiscal outturn versus framework targets, and IMF Article IV consultation outputs.

**Analyst note:** Brazil is not a crisis story for H1 2026 — it is a slow erosion story. The risk for family offices is not the binary event but the cumulative drift in fiscal credibility that re-rates the entire asset complex by 100-200bps over 18-24 months. Position for that distribution, not for the headline.
