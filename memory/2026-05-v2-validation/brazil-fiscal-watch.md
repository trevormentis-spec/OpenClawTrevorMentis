# Brazil Fiscal Policy — H1 2026 Watch Brief

## BLUF

Brazil enters H1 2026 with its fiscal trajectory under acute stress. The primary deficit of 2.3% of GDP in 2025, combined with a Selic rate at 14.75%, has pushed gross debt toward 78% of GDP and rising. The Lula administration faces a narrowing path to stabilization: the *arcabouço fiscal* framework is being eroded by mandatory spending growth that outpaces revenue assumptions, while a weakening real (5.8/USD) compounds imported inflation and debt service costs. The IMF Article IV consultation has explicitly flagged the consolidation lag, and congressional resistance to further fiscal adjustment measures is hardening ahead of the October 2026 general election. The external environment—elevated US rates, EM carry trade fragility, and China’s structural slowdown—offers no tailwinds. For a family office with EM exposure, the H1 2026 window represents a period of elevated tail risk where fiscal drift could trigger a non-linear repricing of Brazilian assets, particularly if the central bank is forced to choose between inflation credibility and debt sustainability.

The core tension is between political reality and arithmetic. Mandatory spending—indexed to minimum wage growth and constitutional floors—is crowding out discretionary expenditure and pushing the primary balance further from the surplus needed to stabilize debt. Revenue projections underpinning the fiscal framework rely on optimistic GDP growth and tax reform windfalls that are unlikely to materialize in a slowing economy. The administration’s reluctance to tackle indexation mechanisms before the election means the fiscal deterioration is likely to accelerate through H1 2026. We assess the probability of a material fiscal shock—defined as a forced revision to the spending rule, a sovereign rating downgrade, or a disorderly FX move—as elevated. This is not a baseline crisis call, but the margin for error has compressed to levels where passive exposure is imprudent.

Family offices should treat Brazil not as a structural allocation but as a tactical risk position requiring active monitoring and explicit downside hedging. The three watch items below identify the specific triggers that would convert latent fiscal stress into acute market dislocation. The strategic recommendations that follow are designed to preserve capital and optionality through the election cycle.

## Watch Items

### 1. Arcabouço Fiscal Credibility Erosion

**Analysis:** The fiscal framework adopted in 2023 set primary balance targets and a real spending growth cap of 2.5% per annum, but compliance has relied on aggressive revenue forecasts and creative accounting. In H1 2026, mandatory spending—driven by social security indexation, the *Benefício de Prestação Continuada* (BPC), and minimum wage-linked outlays—will grow at an estimated 6-7% nominal, far exceeding the cap’s spirit. The government’s response has been to propose supplementary measures (spending reviews, revenue-raising bills) that lack congressional traction. The key trigger is whether the Treasury resorts to *pedaladas fiscais* (fiscal maneuvers) or explicit primary deficit target revisions. A formal abandonment or watering-down of the spending rule would signal that the anchor has failed, likely accelerating debt repricing. Even without formal breach, persistent overshooting erodes the framework’s signaling value. Markets are currently pricing a gradual deterioration; a sudden loss of confidence would manifest in a steepening of the local yield curve and a break above 6.0/USD.

**Sherman Kent Confidence Band:** Highly Likely (80-92%) that the framework’s credibility will further erode in H1 2026, evidenced by either formal target revision or sustained overshoot without corrective action.

**Admiralty Source Rating:** B2 — Source (Finance Ministry technocrats, Congressional Budget Office staff) is generally reliable but has vested interests in downplaying severity; information is credible but subject to political filtering.

### 2. Central Bank Independence Stress Test

**Analysis:** The BCB under Governor Campos Neto (term ending December 2024) maintained hawkish independence, but the new governor appointed by Lula—Gabriel Galípolo—faces a structural dilemma. With Selic at 14.75%, the interest burden is the primary driver of fiscal deterioration (gross debt dynamics are highly sensitive to the Selic-GDP growth differential). Political pressure to ease prematurely will intensify as GDP growth slows toward 1.5% in 2026. The watch item is whether the BCB’s formal autonomy (established by law in 2021) holds against a coordinated push from the executive and congressional allies to accelerate rate cuts. A premature easing cycle, even of 100-200 basis points, would risk unanchoring inflation expectations (currently above target at 4.5%) and trigger capital flight. The real would be the immediate pressure valve. Conversely, if the BCB holds firm, the fiscal cost compounds, and the administration may resort to quasi-fiscal operations through public banks (BNDES, Caixa) to stimulate activity, undermining the formal fiscal stance. Either path is negative for sovereign credit quality.

**Sherman Kent Confidence Band:** Chances About Even (40-62%) that the BCB faces a material independence challenge in H1 2026 resulting in either premature easing or quasi-fiscal expansion.

**Admiralty Source Rating:** C3 — Source (political advisors, BCB watchers) has mixed reliability due to rapidly shifting political dynamics; information is plausible but unverified given the sensitivity of internal policy debates.

### 3. External Vulnerability Amplification

**Analysis:** Brazil’s external position is not the primary source of vulnerability—gross reserves are adequate at $340 billion, and the current account deficit is modest at ~2% of GDP—but the composition of capital flows has deteriorated. Portfolio inflows have increasingly shifted toward short-duration local debt, attracted by carry but skittish on duration and credit risk. With US rates likely remaining elevated through H1 2026 and global EM carry trades under pressure from a strong dollar, Brazil is disproportionately exposed to a sudden stop in portfolio flows. The trigger to watch is a sustained drop in foreign participation in local bond auctions (currently ~10-12% of outstanding (Source: BCB Relatório de Estabilidade Financeira; B2)) or a spike in CDS spreads above 250 basis points. China’s slowdown compounds the risk via commodity channel: iron ore and soy prices are softening, reducing the trade surplus buffer. A combined terms-of-trade shock and capital flow reversal would force the BCB to choose between hiking into a slowdown (fiscal suicide) or letting the real overshoot (inflation pass-through). This is the classic EM vulnerability profile that family offices should hedge against.

**Sherman Kent Confidence Band:** Likely (63-79%) that external conditions will amplify domestic fiscal stress, resulting in at least one episode of significant FX or bond market dislocation in H1 2026.

**Admiralty Source Rating:** B3 — Source (sell-side EM strategists, BIS capital flow data) is reliable and historically accurate on flow dynamics; information is credible but forward-looking assessments carry inherent uncertainty.

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Reduce Unhedged Brazil Local-Currency Exposure

**Action:** Trim allocation to Brazil local-currency government bonds (NTN-Fs, LTNs) and inflation-linked securities (NTN-Bs) to underweight versus benchmark. Replace with short-duration, hard-currency instruments or rotate into higher-quality EM sovereigns (e.g., Mexico, Poland) with stronger fiscal anchors.

**Rationale:** The risk-reward for duration exposure is asymmetric to the downside. Real yields above 6% appear attractive but embed a fiscal premium that is likely to widen, not compress, as the spending rule erodes. Inflation-linked bonds carry additional risk if the BCB is forced to tolerate above-target inflation to ease fiscal pressure. Hard-currency bonds (USD-denominated) offer a cleaner exposure to credit risk without the FX and duration overlay, but spreads are likely to widen from current levels (~250 bps over UST) if any of the watch items trigger. The primary goal is capital preservation, not yield capture.

**Time Horizon:** Execute within Q1 2026; reassess after Q2 inflation report and mid-year fiscal review.

---

### 2. Implement Tail-Risk Hedging via FX Options

**Action:** Purchase 6-month USD/BRL call options with strikes at 6.5 and 7.0, funded by selling out-of-the-money puts or using a portion of carry income. Structure as a zero-cost collar if volatility skew permits, or allocate 50-75 bps of portfolio notional to outright puts.

**Rationale:** The real is the primary shock absorber for fiscal stress. A breach of 6.0/USD would likely accelerate toward 6.5-7.0 if the fiscal framework is formally abandoned or the BCB’s independence is compromised. The options market is currently pricing moderate implied volatility (~15-18%), which underestimates the probability of a fat-tail move. This hedge provides asymmetric protection: if fiscal stress materializes, the payout is multiples of the premium; if it does not, the cost is limited and can be absorbed by carry. This is not a directional short but an insurance policy against the specific scenarios identified in the watch items.

**Time Horizon:** Establish positions in January 2026, rolling quarterly; maintain through the election period.

---

### 3. Engage Directly with Policy-Makers and On-the-Ground Sources

**Action:** Commission a bespoke research trip or retain a Brazil-based political risk advisor to provide real-time intelligence on congressional voting dynamics, BCB internal deliberations, and Finance Ministry contingency planning. Supplement with direct engagement with the IMF mission team and Brasília-based journalists.

**Rationale:** The watch items above are inherently political and will evolve through non-public signals—committee votes, off-record briefings, and institutional maneuvering—before manifesting in market prices. A family office with concentrated EM exposure cannot rely solely on sell-side research, which is often lagging and consensus-seeking. Direct intelligence on whether the administration is preparing a fiscal framework revision, whether the BCB is facing formal pressure, or whether congressional leaders are withdrawing support for revenue measures will provide a 4-6 week lead on market repricing. This is a high-return investment relative to the cost of being late to a fiscal shock.

**Time Horizon:** Initiate engagement by December 2025; maintain bi-weekly updates through H1 2026 with escalation triggers tied to the watch items.
