### 1. 12-Month Security and Political Risk Picture, Broken by Sub-Corridor

The Bajío corridor is not a single risk pool. It is three distinct security and political ecosystems, each with a different risk profile for industrial real estate. The 12-month outlook is dominated by the aftermath of the February 22, 2026 killing of CJNG leader El Mencho and the subsequent succession struggle, which has already triggered a wave of violence in Guanajuato and spillover into Aguascalientes. Querétaro remains the outlier—more stable but not immune.

**Guanajuato Sub-Corridor (León, Irapuato, Silao, Celaya, Salamanca)**
- **Risk Level: High / Deteriorating.** This is the epicenter of the post-Mencho violence. The February 2026 Jalisco operation aftermath produced 70+ attacks across 23 municipalities, 60 arson incidents, and damage to over 200 Oxxo stores [El Financiero, Feb-Mar 2026, B2-C1; Lieber Institute, Feb 2026, B2]. The CJNG succession battle—El Pelón vs. internal rivals—is being fought on Guanajuato’s industrial corridors. Extortion of logistics firms and construction material suppliers is **almost certain (>85%)** to increase as factions seek revenue. The state government’s capacity to respond is degraded; the Sheinbaum administration’s security strategy under SSPC Harfuch has not yet produced a dedicated Bajío task force [Wilson Center, Feb 2026, B2].
- **Judgment:** **Probable (50-60%)** that at least one major industrial park (e.g., Silao’s automotive cluster or Irapuato’s logistics hub) will experience a temporary shutdown due to cartel-related violence within 12 months. **Unlikely (<15%)** that the state will regain control of key highways (e.g., Fed 45, Fed 110) from extortion checkpoints.
- **Subscriber Action Line:** **Do not commit new capital to Guanajuato industrial assets until Q4 2026 at earliest.** If existing exposure exists, secure 24/7 private security for all construction sites and logistics routes. **Flag:** No municipal-level cartel control maps exist for Guanajuato’s industrial corridors [GAP: No geography files in registry].

**Aguascalientes Sub-Corridor (Aguascalientes City, Jesús María, Rincón de Romos)**
- **Risk Level: Moderate / Watch.** Aguascalientes has historically been a security outlier in the Bajío, but the February 2026 crisis saw roads burned by criminal groups for the first time in recent memory [El Financiero, Feb 22 2026, C2]. This is a **significant downgrade signal**. The state’s industrial parks (e.g., Nissan’s complex, the logistics corridor to Zacatecas) are now within reach of CJNG splinter groups operating from Guanajuato’s northern fringe. The state government has reinforced border security with Guanajuato, but this is reactive, not preventive.
- **Judgment:** **Even chance (~50%)** that Aguascalientes experiences a material security event (arson, highway blockade, or extortion campaign targeting industrial tenants) within 12 months. **Likely (60-85%)** that insurance premiums for industrial properties in the state rise by 15-25% in the next renewal cycle.
- **Subscriber Action Line:** **Proceed with caution.** Favor assets inside Aguascalientes City’s urban perimeter over exurban industrial parks. **Require** force majeure clauses covering cartel-related disruption in all lease agreements. **GAP:** No per-municipality extortion or homicide rates for Aguascalientes industrial corridors are available in our registry.

**Querétaro Sub-Corridor (Querétaro City, San Juan del Río, El Marqués)**
- **Risk Level: Low / Stable but Strained.** Querétaro remains the safest Bajío sub-corridor, reinforced by its own security cordon at the Guanajuato and Michoacán borders [El Financiero, Feb 22 2026, C2]. The state has invested in police intelligence and has a functioning C5 security center. However, the post-Mencho violence is pushing criminal groups northward; Querétaro is now a **likely (60-85%)** destination for displaced CJNG elements seeking new extortion territories. The state’s industrial parks (e.g., Bernardo Quintana, El Marqués) are prime targets because of high-value logistics and automotive tenants.
- **Judgment:** **Probable (50-60%)** that Querétaro sees a 20-30% increase in cargo theft and extortion attempts targeting industrial tenants within 12 months. **Highly unlikely (<15%)** that the state experiences the same level of arson or open attacks as Guanajuato.
- **Subscriber Action Line:** **This is your best entry point for the first $50-75M of exposure.** Prioritize assets in Querétaro City’s established industrial corridors with existing security infrastructure. **Do not** assume Querétaro’s stability is permanent—build in a 12-month security review clause.

**Political Risk (Cross-Corridor)**
- **Sheinbaum Administration:** The security strategy under SSPC Harfuch is still in formulation. No dedicated Bajío task force has been announced. The administration’s focus remains on Jalisco and Michoacán. **Likely (60-85%)** that the federal government will not commit significant resources to Guanajuato until after the USMCA review (July 1, 2026) is resolved, as political capital is being conserved.
- **USMCA Review 2026:** The July 1 deadline is a **critical political risk factor**. A negative outcome (e.g., U.S. imposition of tariffs on Mexican industrial goods due to security failures) would **almost certainly (>85%)** reduce nearshoring demand for Bajío industrial space. The Sheinbaum administration is aware of this and is likely to make symbolic security gestures, but substantive change is **unlikely (<15%)** before the deadline.
- **Subscriber Action Line:** **Hedge USMCA risk directly** (see Section 3). **Do not** assume any political stability in Guanajuato or Aguascalientes for the next 12 months.

### 2. Three to Five Specific Developments to Watch for Upgrade/Downgrade

**Development 1: CJNG Succession Resolution (El Pelón vs. Rivals)**
- **What to Watch:** Whether El Pelón consolidates control or a multi-faction war erupts. The February 2026 violence in Guanajuato is a direct result of the succession vacuum. If El Pelón secures the organization by Q3 2026, violence may **downgrade** to pre-February levels (still high, but predictable). If a multi-front war emerges, **upgrade** to a full-scale Bajío crisis.
- **Source:** Wilson Center, Feb 2026 [B2]; Lieber Institute, Feb 2026 [B2].
- **Upgrade Signal:** More than 100 attacks per month in Guanajuato for two consecutive months. **Almost certain (>85%)** this would trigger capital flight from the corridor.
- **Downgrade Signal:** A public truce or succession announcement by CJNG leadership, followed by a 30% reduction in attacks. **Unlikely (<15%)** in the next 6 months.
- **Subscriber Action Line:** **Monitor weekly.** If attacks in Guanajuato exceed 100/month for two months, **immediately pause all Bajío commitments** and shift capital to Querétaro or northern Mexico (Nuevo León).

- **What to Watch:** The U.S. Trade Representative’s assessment of Mexico’s security and labor enforcement. A negative finding could trigger tariffs on Bajío industrial goods (autos, auto parts, electronics). This is the single largest macro risk to your $200M thesis.
- **Source:** USMCA review timeline, 2026 [A2]; Delcor/Líder Empresarial, Mar 2026 [B2].
- **Upgrade Signal:** U.S. announces a 90-day extension for negotiations. **Probable (50-60%)** given political incentives on both sides.
- **Downgrade Signal:** U.S. imposes tariffs of 10% or more on Mexican industrial goods. **Unlikely (15-40%)** but catastrophic if realized.
- **Subscriber Action Line:** **Buy protection on the Mexican peso (MXN) and short Mexican industrial ETFs (e.g., EWW) for the July 1 window.** This is a direct hedge against the worst-case USMCA outcome.

**Development 3: Harfuch’s Bajío Task Force Announcement**
- **What to Watch:** Whether SSPC Harfuch announces a dedicated federal security task force for Guanajuato and Aguascalientes. This would signal federal commitment and could **downgrade** security risk in those sub-corridors.

**Development 4: Aguascalientes Border Security Breach**
- **What to Watch:** Whether criminal groups from Guanajuato successfully establish a permanent extortion corridor into Aguascalientes’ industrial parks. The February 2026 road burnings were a warning shot.
- **Source:** El Financiero, Feb 22 2026 [C2].
- **Upgrade Signal:** A second road burning or an extortion campaign targeting Nissan’s supply chain. **Probable (50-60%)** within 12 months.
- **Downgrade Signal:** Aguascalientes state police successfully interdict a CJNG cell. **Even chance (~50%)**.

**Development 5: Bajío Industrial Construction Start Data (Leading Indicator)**
