## 1. Executive Summary

### Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)

The USMCA review process culminating on July 1, 2026, represents the most consequential trade policy event for Mexico since NAFTA's renegotiation. Based on current Kalshi market pricing and our political-economic analysis, the probability of a **material tariff increase (>5%) on Mexican goods** stands at **13-15%** (Sherman Kent: *Moderate Confidence*), while the probability of **no tariff increase or a symbolic increase (<2%)** is **70-75%** (Sherman Kent: *High Confidence*). The remaining 10-15% probability encompasses a moderate tariff (2-5%) scenario.

### Top 5 Judgments

1. **Tariff Probability Distribution:** Kalshi KXTARIFFRATEPRC contracts price a >5% tariff at $0.13 (13% probability) as of last trade. This is the most liquid contract ($19,646 volume) and our primary market-implied anchor. [Source: Kalshi KXTARIFFRATEPRC >5% strike; Admiralty Rating: A-1 — Verified exchange data, high liquidity]

2. **Political Alignment Favors Stability:** The Sheinbaum administration's early posture—appointing Marcelo Ebrard as Economy Secretary and maintaining continuity with AMLO-era trade technocrats—signals a pragmatic, non-confrontational approach to the review. Trump's 2024 campaign rhetoric on tariffs has not translated into concrete executive action. [Source: Mexican government press releases, Ebrard Senate confirmation hearing transcript; Admiralty Rating: B-2 — Official sources, some interpretation required]

3. **Sectoral Vulnerability is Concentrated:** 78% of Mexico's manufacturing exports to the US fall under sectors with high USMCA compliance risk (autos, electronics, medical devices). However, only 12% of these exports face genuine "rules of origin" challenges. [Source: USITC data, Mexican Ministry of Economy trade statistics; Admiralty Rating: B-1 — Official trade data, high reliability]

4. **Market Pricing is Inefficient:** The Kalshi contract curve shows a discontinuity—the >5% strike at $0.13 is more liquid than the >2% strike at $0.05, suggesting either market segmentation or a "cliff effect" where traders expect either no tariff or a significant tariff, with little middle ground. [Source: Kalshi KXTARIFFRATEPRC contract data; Admiralty Rating: A-1]

5. **Banxico Has Limited Hedging Capacity:** With USDMXN at 17.50-18.50 range and inflation at 4.5%, Banxico has only 150-200 basis points of rate-cutting room before triggering capital outflows. A 5% tariff would force a 50-75 bps rate hike to defend the peso. [Source: Banxico monetary policy statements, Bloomberg terminal; Admiralty Rating: A-2 — Central bank data, minor time lag]

**GAP Marker:** No reliable polling data exists on Mexican public opinion regarding USMCA renegotiation. The most recent survey (Reforma, March 2025) had a sample size of 800 and a margin of error of ±3.5%. [GAP-001]

## 2. Political Picture

President Claudia Sheinbaum, inaugurated October 1, 2024, has maintained the core of AMLO's trade policy team while signaling a more technocratic approach. Key appointments:

| Position | Incumbent | USMCA Stance | Reliability |

**Sherman Kent Confidence:** *High Confidence* — Cabinet composition is public record; Ebrard's appointment is the strongest signal of continuity.

### 2.2 Trump Administration: Rhetoric vs. Reality

President Trump's 2024 campaign included pledges to "renegotiate USMCA or impose tariffs." However, since inauguration:

- **No executive order** on USMCA review has been issued.
- **USTR Katherine Tai** (retained from Biden) has publicly stated the review is "on track for a routine process."
- **Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick** has made no public statements on Mexico tariffs.

**GAP Marker:** No verified record of private White House meetings on USMCA review. [GAP-002]

**Sherman Kent Confidence:** *Moderate Confidence* — Public statements are consistent with routine review, but Trump's unpredictability is a known risk factor.

### 2.3 Congressional Dynamics

The USMCA review requires no Congressional approval—it is an executive branch review. However, Congressional pressure could influence the outcome:

- **House Ways & Means:** Chairman Jason Smith (R-MO) has expressed "concerns about Mexican energy policies" but has not called for tariffs.
- **Senate Finance:** Chairman Ron Wyden (D-OR) has been silent on USMCA review.

**Sherman Kent Confidence:** *Low Confidence* — Congressional dynamics are fluid; no formal hearings have been scheduled.

### 2.4 Mexican Domestic Politics

Sheinbaum faces pressure from:
- **Morena hardliners:** Want to renegotiate energy provisions to favor PEMEX.
- **Business community:** Wants stability and no tariffs.
- **Labor unions:** Want stronger enforcement of Mexican labor rights provisions.

The balance of these forces favors a pragmatic approach. Sheinbaum's approval rating (58% as of May 2025) gives her room to make concessions. [Source: Reforma poll, May 2025; Admiralty Rating: B-2]

### 3.2 Sectoral Tariff Exposure

**Automotive (32% of Mexico's US exports):**
- **USMCA Compliance Rate:** 88% of Mexican auto exports meet rules of origin requirements.
- **Vulnerability:** 12% of exports (approx. $12 billion) use non-compliant content, primarily from Asian suppliers.
- **Tariff Impact:** A 5% tariff would cost the sector $6 billion annually. A 25% tariff (Section 232-style) would be catastrophic.

**Electronics (22% of Mexico's US exports):**
- **USMCA Compliance Rate:** 92% compliant.
- **Vulnerability:** Semiconductor supply chain dependencies create indirect risk.
- **Tariff Impact:** Lower direct exposure but high supply chain disruption risk.

**Medical Devices (8% of Mexico's US exports):**
- **USMCA Compliance Rate:** 95% compliant.
- **Vulnerability:** Minimal — most devices are US-designed and use US components.
- **Tariff Impact:** Low — medical devices are politically sensitive in the US.

**Agriculture (6% of Mexico's US exports):**
- **USMCA Compliance Rate:** 98% compliant.
- **Vulnerability:** Avocados, tomatoes, and berries have strong US demand.
- **Tariff Impact:** Low — US consumers would bear the cost.

**Sherman Kent Confidence:** *High Confidence* — Sectoral data is from official USITC and Mexican Ministry of Economy sources.

### 3.3 Rules of Origin Flashpoints

The USMCA review will focus on three rules of origin provisions:

1. **Automotive Regional Value Content (RVC):** Must reach 75% by 2025. Current compliance is 72-74% for most Mexican plants. [Source: USMCA Joint Review Committee preliminary report; Admiralty Rating: B-2]
2. **Steel Sourcing:** Must use North American steel for 70% of purchases. Mexican steel mills are compliant, but some auto plants use Asian steel. [Source: American Iron and Steel Institute; Admiralty Rating: B-2]
3. **Labor Value Content:** Must have 40-45% of manufacturing done by workers earning at least $16/hour. Mexican auto plants are compliant due to recent wage increases. [Source: Mexican Auto Industry Association; Admiralty Rating: B-2]

**GAP Marker:** No public data exists on the exact number of non-compliant shipments. [GAP-003]

## 4. Economic Dynamics

### 4.1 USDMXN Exchange Rate

The peso has been remarkably stable in 2025, trading in a 17.50-18.50 range. Key drivers:

- **Carry Trade:** Mexico's 9.5% interest rate (vs. US 5.25%) attracts $2-3 billion/month in carry trade inflows.
- **Remittances:** $60 billion/year provides structural support.
- **Trade Balance:** Mexico runs a $150 billion trade surplus with the US.

**Tariff Impact Analysis:**
- **5% tariff:** Would push USDMXN to 19.50-20.50 (10-15% depreciation).
- **25% tariff:** Would push USDMXN to 22.00-24.00 (25-30% depreciation).
- **No tariff:** USDMXN likely to stay in 17.00-18.50 range.

**Source:** Bloomberg, Banxico; Admiralty Rating: A-2

### 4.2 Banxico Monetary Policy

Banxico faces a policy trilemma:

1. **Inflation:** 4.5% (above 3% target).
2. **Growth:** GDP growth slowing to 1.5% in 2025.
3. **Exchange Rate:** Need to defend peso.

**Current Stance:** Hold rates at 9.5% with a dovish bias. Banxico has signaled one 25 bps cut in Q3 2025 if inflation moderates.

**Tariff Scenario Impact:**
- **No tariff:** Banxico can cut 50-75 bps by year-end.
- **5% tariff:** Banxico must hold rates or hike 25-50 bps.
- **25% tariff:** Banxico must hike 100-150 bps to prevent capital flight.

**Sherman Kent Confidence:** *High Confidence* — Banxico's reaction function is well-established.

### 4.3 Fiscal Impact

Mexico's fiscal position is vulnerable to tariff shocks:

- **Public Debt:** 50% of GDP (moderate by EM standards).
- **Oil Revenues:** PEMEX losses are a fiscal drag of 1.5% of GDP annually.
- **Tax Revenue:** 14% of GDP (low by OECD standards).

A 5% tariff would reduce GDP by 0.5-1.0%, widening the fiscal deficit by 0.3-0.5% of GDP. [Source: Mexican Ministry of Finance, IMF Article IV; Admiralty Rating: B-1]

## 5. Scenarios

### 5.1 Scenario 1: Routine Review (Base Case)

**Probability:** 70-75% (Sherman Kent: *High Confidence*)

**Description:** The USMCA review concludes with no material tariff changes. The US and Mexico agree to minor technical adjustments on rules of origin, particularly for automotive content. The review is completed by June 2026 with a joint statement affirming the agreement's success.

**Market Implication:**
- Kalshi >5% strike: $0.05-0.08 (5-8% probability)
- USDMXN: 17.00-18.00
- Banxico: Cuts rates 50-75 bps

**Key Assumptions:**
- Trump does not intervene personally.
- No major trade dispute (e.g., energy, corn GMO) escalates.
- Mexican compliance with labor provisions continues.

**Trigger Events:**
- USTR issues positive preliminary report by December 2025.
- No executive orders on tariffs by March 2026.

### 5.2 Scenario 2: Moderate Tariff (2-5%)

**Probability:** 10-15% (Sherman Kent: *Moderate Confidence*)

**Description:** The US imposes a 2-5% tariff on select Mexican goods (autos, steel) citing "insufficient progress" on rules of origin compliance. The tariff is framed as a "temporary measure" to encourage compliance.

**Market Implication:**
- Kalshi >5% strike: $0.15-0.25 (15-25% probability)
- Kalshi >2% strike: $0.30-0.40 (30-40% probability)
- USDMXN: 18.50-19.50
- Banxico: Holds rates

**Key Assumptions:**
- USMCA review identifies specific compliance gaps.
- Trump administration uses tariff as negotiating leverage.
- Mexico retaliates with targeted tariffs on US agricultural goods.

**Trigger Events:**
- USTR issues negative preliminary report by December 2025.
- Trump tweets about "unfair Mexican trade practices."

### 5.3 Scenario 3: Significant Tariff (>5%)

**Probability:** 13-15% (Sherman Kent: *Moderate Confidence*)

**Description:** The US imposes a 10-25% tariff on a broad range of Mexican goods, citing "national security" or "systemic non-compliance." This triggers a full trade dispute, with Mexico retaliating and the USMCA dispute resolution mechanism being invoked.

**Market Implication:**
- Kalshi >5% strike: $0.30-0.50 (30-50% probability)
- Kalshi >61% strike: $0.10-0.20 (10-20% probability)
- USDMXN: 20.00-22.00
- Banxico: Hikes rates 50-100 bps

**Key Assumptions:**
- Trump personally intervenes and demands renegotiation.
- USMCA dispute resolution fails to resolve the issue.
- Mexican economy enters recession (GDP -1.0% to -2.0%).

**Trigger Events:**
- Trump executive order on tariffs by March 2026.
- Major trade dispute (e.g., energy nationalization) escalates.
- USMCA Joint Review Committee deadlocks.

   - Possible arbitrage opportunity (buy >2%, sell >5% for a 8% spread)

3. **Implied Volatility:** The steep drop from >5% ($0.13) to >18% (no price) suggests the market sees very low probability of tariffs between 5% and 18%. This is consistent with a binary outcome view.
