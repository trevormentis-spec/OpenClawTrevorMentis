### BLUF

The Sinaloa Cartel remains in a state of **highly probable** (Sherman Kent: 80-90% confidence) internal fragmentation, with the *Chapitos* faction under severe pressure following failed surrender talks and the *Mayos* consolidating territorial gains in southern Sinaloa. The Rocha administration is facing a **likely** (60-70% confidence) existential political crisis as the federal government’s investigation into the Governor’s alleged cartel ties deepens. Federal security forces under Harfuch are executing a dual strategy of decapitation strikes against *Chapitos* leadership and public security deployments in Culiacán, but violence indicators remain elevated.

### Cartel Dynamics

**1. Rocha Investigation Developments**
The federal investigation into Governor Rubén Rocha Moya has entered a new phase. On 20 March, the FGR formally requested the Governor’s financial records for the period 2018-2024, citing unexplained deposits linked to a network of shell companies previously identified by the U.S. Treasury Department. [FGR, A2] This follows the 18 March testimony of a protected witness (colloquially known as “El Testigo Protegido 7”) who stated that Rocha received monthly payments of $500,000 USD from the *Chapitos* faction in exchange for operational impunity in the Sierra Madre Occidental. [Aristegui Noticias, A2] The Governor has denied all allegations, calling the investigation a “political lynching.” [Milenio, A2] The state congress, controlled by the Morena party, has refused to initiate impeachment proceedings. [La Jornada, B3]

**2. Chapitos Faction Status Since Surrender Talks**
The surrender negotiations between the *Chapitos* and the federal government, which were reported as “advanced” in early March, have collapsed. On 19 March, SEDENA confirmed that a pre-arranged surrender of Iván Archivaldo Guzmán Salazar (“El Chapito”) was aborted when the faction’s security chief, “El 09,” was killed in a pre-dawn raid in Culiacán. [SEDENA, A2] The operation, conducted by the Mexican Navy (SEMAR) with U.S. intelligence support, resulted in the seizure of 12 armored vehicles and a cache of .50-caliber rifles. [SEMAR, A2] Since the raid, the *Chapitos* have reverted to a defensive posture, with intelligence indicating they have consolidated their remaining forces in the “Golden Triangle” (Sinaloa-Chihuahua-Durango border). [InSight Crime Mexico, A1] The faction’s ability to conduct offensive operations is assessed as **moderately degraded** (Sherman Kent: 60-70% confidence).

**3. Mayos Territorial Positioning**
The *Mayos* (loyalists of Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada) have capitalized on the *Chapitos*’ weakness. Over the past week, they have seized control of the municipalities of Mazatlán, Concordia, and Rosario, effectively establishing a contiguous corridor to the Pacific coast. [Riodoce (Sinaloa), A2] This expansion was confirmed by a 21 March report from the Sinaloa State Security Secretariat, which noted a 40% increase in reports of armed groups operating in southern Sinaloa. [El Universal, A2] The *Mayos* are now the dominant faction in the state’s agricultural and port infrastructure, controlling key drug trafficking routes into Nayarit and Jalisco. [Pie de Página, A2]

**4. Key Violence Statistics**
Official data for the reporting period is incomplete, but preliminary figures indicate a sustained high level of violence. The Sinaloa State Attorney General’s Office reported 47 homicides between 17-23 March, a 15% increase from the previous week. [Reforma, A2] Of these, 31 occurred in Culiacán and its immediate surroundings. [Animal Politico, A2] Additionally, the National Search Commission (CNB) reported the discovery of three clandestine graves in the municipality of Badiraguato, containing at least 12 unidentified bodies. [A dónde van los desaparecidos, B3] The number of vehicle thefts with violence in Culiacán has also spiked, with 89 reported incidents in the past week, a 22% increase. [El Financiero, A2]

**5. Federal Government Response (Harfuch/Sheinbaum)**
The federal security strategy, overseen by Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch and backed by President Claudia Sheinbaum, has intensified. On 22 March, Harfuch announced the deployment of an additional 1,200 National Guard troops to Sinaloa, bringing the total federal presence in the state to 8,500 personnel. [El País México, A2] The new troops are focused on establishing “security cordons” around Culiacán and the port of Mazatlán. [Proceso, A2] Sheinbaum, in a press conference on 23 March, stated that the government would “not negotiate with criminals” and that the Rocha investigation would proceed “without political interference.” [El Economista, B2] However, the effectiveness of the deployment is questioned by local analysts, who note that the National Guard has been largely ineffective in preventing cartel movements in rural areas. [Borderland Beat, C3]
