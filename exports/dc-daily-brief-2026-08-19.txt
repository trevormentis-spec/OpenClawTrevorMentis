═══ BLUF ═══

Transformer lead times have just worsened to 18–24 months for >50 MVA units, PJM’s interconnection queue is 122 GW, and new DC builds face 4–6 year delays. Satellite imagery shows no new anomalies across 25 Tier 1 hubs, so the real risk is compounding quietly on the power side.

═══ SUbSEA CABLE INTELLIGENCE ═══
No active cable cuts, repairs, or outage flags in the current registry. Tracked: 694 subsea cables, 1,917 landing stations. The US Eastern Seaboard — New York/New Jersey and Ashburn — remains heavily dependent on transatlantic cables landing in New Jersey and Virginia Beach; San Jose and Los Angeles depend on transpacific cables landing in California and Oregon. The 1,917 landing stations remain the long-tail single points of failure: a construction cut at a major landing station would ripple into four to five top clusters. No new cable announcements or maintenance events flagged.

═══ POWER & GRID ═══
Grid is the dominant risk driver. US hyperscale capacity is 1,563 MW — more than seven times the next market (GB: 219 MW). All eight highest-risk facilities are Equinix US sites with driver=grid: Ashburn, Dallas, San Jose, Chicago, Los Angeles, Newark, New York (111 8th Ave), and New York (60 Hudson). PJM interconnection queue: 122 GW, with new builds facing 4–6 year delays. Transformer lead times are 18–24 months for >50 MVA units — the single biggest constraint. Switchgear is 12–18 months, generators 8–12 months. Expect generator-only operations or forced load-shedding during peak heat events in Ashburn/NoVA and Dallas. No energy price breakout today, but scarcity pricing risk is elevated in PJM and ERCOT.

Australia (Sydney) is in winter with no tropical threat. No active storm landfall or seismic event in the registry, but operators should have fuel contracts and flood gates tested.
