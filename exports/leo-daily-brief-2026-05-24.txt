Upcoming launch manifests tell a story of unbroken Starlink momentum. Seven Starlink-dedicated Falcon 9 flights appear in the database, with three imminent: Group 10-47 (25 May), Group 17-37 (26 May), and Group 10-53 (29 May). Each likely delivers ~22 satellites, adding roughly 150–200 spacecraft by month-end. This pace—approximately two launches per week—will push the constellation past 9,600 spacecraft, within striking distance of the 12,000-satellite Phase 1 target for initial operational capability. For ground capacity, each new orbital shell demands additional gateway diversity, especially in underserved latitude bands. However, SpaceX’s integrated model means that most of this buildout will be internal; the company’s 38 ground sites in our register already constitute the largest single-operator footprint. The Chinese Long March 7A and crewed Shenzhou missions are peripheral to ground-station demand unless they herald a new LEO broadband system, which remains unconfirmed.

OneWeb’s 10 sites and Telesat’s 3 sites show no momentum; their launch schedules are quiet. Kuiper, with only 5 registered sites, is still in early deployment but will need a rapid buildout once Project Kuiper satellite launches begin in earnest. The launch data therefore signals a widening gap between the Starlink behemoth and its pursuers, with direct consequences for the addressable third-party ground-station market.
