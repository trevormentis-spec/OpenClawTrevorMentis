## Executive Summary

The global supply of RDX (Research Department Explosive / cyclotrimethylenetrinitramine) and its derivative formulations — most prominently Composition C-4 — is in the early stages of a structural transition from a peacetime equilibrium to a sustained wartime mobilization footing. Demand is being pulled simultaneously by three vectors: Ukrainian artillery replenishment, NATO stockpile reconstitution post-2022, and Indo-Pacific deterrence preparations by the United States and treaty allies. Supply, meanwhile, remains concentrated in a small number of fragile single-point-of-failure facilities — Holston (US), Eurenco Bergerac (France), Chemring Nobel (Norway), Nitro-Chem (Poland for TNT), and Sverdlov (Russia) — most of which were sized for a Cold War-residual demand profile.

We assess with **high confidence** that physical RDX output, not financial capital or political will, is the binding constraint on Western 155mm artillery shell production through at least end-2026 and **likely** (60–80%) through 2027. We further assess with **moderate confidence** that the Ukrainian strike campaign of February–May 2026 has materially degraded Russian RDX/HMX production capacity, though precise damage assessments remain uncertain pending further imagery analysis.

This report is the first formal validation pass on this topic; estimates carry corresponding uncertainty and should be re-baselined quarterly.

### 1.2 Three Demand Drivers

**Driver 1 — Ukrainian Artillery Replenishment.** Sustained Ukrainian 155mm expenditure has been the dominant short-cycle demand pull since 2022. Public reporting (Admiralty B-2) indicates Ukrainian and allied 155mm consumption running at the upper end of NATO industrial capacity.

**Driver 2 — NATO Stockpile Reconstitution.** European member states are simultaneously refilling depleted national stockpiles and meeting new alliance targets. The EU's Act in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP) provides direct co-financing for capacity expansion. Rheinmetall, KNDS, Nammo, and BAE Systems Land are all in multi-year capex cycles.

**Driver 3 — Indo-Pacific Deterrence Posture.** US precision-guided munitions stockpile reviews (Admiralty B-2) suggest a deliberate buildup of long-range fires inventories for Pacific contingencies. RDX is feedstock not only for artillery but for nearly all major Western insensitive-munition warhead fills (PBXN-series, IMX-series), tying naval and aerospace demand to the same upstream bottleneck.

**Net assessment:** All three drivers are likely (60–80%) to remain active simultaneously through at least 2028. This is a structurally different demand environment than any since the late Cold War, and existing producer-capacity plans **may not** be sized appropriately even after announced expansions complete.

## 2. Producer Landscape

### 2.1 North America

**Holston Army Ammunition Plant (Kingsport, Tennessee)** is the sole United States producer of military-grade RDX and HMX. It is government-owned, contractor-operated (GOCO) by BAE Systems Ordnance Systems Inc. Current production stands at approximately 8 million pounds RDX/year, with a publicly announced expansion path to ~15 million pounds/year (Admiralty B-2 from DoD and BAE communications).

Two new flexible energetic materials (FEM) RDX production trains, each rated at approximately 4 million pounds of material capacity, are under construction. Modernization of the 1943-vintage Weak Acetic Acid Recovery Plant is underway — a critical and underappreciated piece of work, as acetic acid recovery is central to the economics and environmental footprint of the Bachmann process.

A separate "Plant X" concept — a new standalone RDX/HMX facility documented in a Nuclear Threat Reduction Laboratory (NTRL) report — has been identified but is **years from operational** status. We assess Plant X is unlikely (15–35%) to contribute material output before 2030 even under accelerated funding.

**Bottom line:** Holston is the single most important node in the entire Western energetic-materials supply chain. Any sustained disruption — fire, regulatory event, labor action — would cascade into US artillery, naval, and air-delivered munitions programs simultaneously.

### 2.2 Europe

**Eurenco Bergerac (France)** has re-emerged as the European RDX and propellant anchor following recovery from the 2022 nitrocellulose plant accident. A new propellant line became operational in January 2025, and the French government has committed approximately €500M for artillery propellant capacity (Admiralty A-2).

**Nitro-Chem (Bydgoszcz, Poland)** is the **sole NATO TNT producer**. This is a remarkable and underreported strategic concentration. Nitro-Chem announced a second production line in March 2026 and signed a US TNT supply agreement in 2025 (Admiralty B-2). The single-point-of-failure risk here is comparable to Holston for RDX.

**Chemring Nobel (Norway)** is one of two European advanced military explosives producers. A £32M+ expansion is underway, claimed to more than double capacity (Admiralty B-2). A feasibility study for an additional new facility concluded in late 2024; downstream decisions are pending and constitute a key watch item.

**Rheinmetall Várpalota (Hungary)**, a 150-hectare greenfield ammunition plant, is being commissioned in early 2026 for 155mm and 120mm production. We assess with **moderate confidence** that series production at Várpalota is achievable in late 2026 or 2027.

European nitrocellulose capacity is estimated at 4,500–10,000 t/yr against a target near 20,000 t/yr — covered in Section 3.

### 2.3 Russia

Pre-2022, Russia operated an integrated explosives industrial base with **Sverdlov Plant (Dzerzhinsk)** as the principal RDX/HMX node (~8,000 t/yr capacity, Admiralty B-3). The April 30, 2026 strike on Sverdlov, combined with strikes on Promsintez (Samara), Bryansk Chemical Plant, Samara Kommunar, and Krasnozavodsk, has — in our **moderate-confidence** assessment — degraded aggregate Russian military explosives production by a meaningful but imprecisely quantified margin. See Section 5.

### 2.4 Asia-Pacific

**Hanwha Corporation (South Korea)**, founded in 1952 as Korea Explosives Co., is a major RDX/HMX producer with an aggressive export posture (Admiralty B-2). Hanwha has positioned itself as a near-peer second-source for 155mm propellant and energetics to European customers.

**Solar Industries (India)** is a private-sector RDX/HMX/TNT producer exporting to 25+ countries, with deepening role under India's Atmanirbhar Bharat (self-reliance) industrial policy (Admiralty B-3). Solar's capacity growth rate is among the highest in the industry, though absolute scale relative to Holston/Eurenco remains unclear from open sources.

## 3. The Structural Shortage

### 3.1 The European Nitrocellulose Gap

European nitrocellulose (NC) production capacity is estimated at **4,500–10,000 t/yr against a stated requirement of approximately 20,000 t/yr** (Admiralty B-3, with the wide range itself reflecting genuine uncertainty in reported figures). NC is the principal binder/energetic component of artillery propellants; without it, RDX cannot be loaded into deliverable rounds at scale.

The gap is **likely** (60–80%) to persist through 2027 even with announced expansions. The bottleneck is twofold:

1. **Nitration capacity** — physical NC plants, requiring multi-year permitting and construction.
2. **Cotton linter feedstock** — >70% of European cotton linter imports originate in China (Admiralty B-3). This is a structural strategic vulnerability that no announced European program currently resolves.

BAE Systems is developing novel manufacturing technology that would eliminate NC and nitroglycerine from the propellant chain (Admiralty B-2). The technology is at pilot stage, with industrial maturity projected for late 2026. We assess with **low-to-moderate confidence** (25–45%) that this technology will reach meaningful production volumes before 2028.

### 3.2 The Holston Bottleneck and the 100K/Month Shell Target

The US Army's stated objective of 100,000 155mm shells per month — originally targeted for October 2025 — has been delayed. We assess with **moderate confidence** that the target is achievable in Q4 2026, gated by Holston RDX throughput rather than by metal-parts, loading, or assembly capacity.

The RDX bottleneck has been confirmed in multiple Army and DoD public statements (Admiralty A-2). Until the Holston FEM trains are fully online and qualified, every other expansion in the US 155mm value chain is upstream-constrained.

### 3.3 The Plant X Concept

"Plant X" refers to a documented but not-yet-funded standalone US RDX/HMX facility. Its strategic logic is sound: a second-source domestic node would eliminate the Holston single-point-of-failure risk. Its programmatic status is opaque. Whether Plant X appears as a line item in the FY2027 President's Budget is one of our top five 90-day watch signals (Section 6).

### 3.4 TNT Single-Source Risk

That **Nitro-Chem in Bydgoszcz is the only NATO TNT producer** is the most underappreciated single-point-of-failure in Western energetics. While substitutes exist for some applications (IMX-series formulations), the legacy installed base of TNT-dependent munitions across NATO is enormous. The Swebal AB (Sweden) new TNT plant targeting 2028 (Admiralty B-3) is a partial mitigation but does not arrive in time to relieve current pressure. We assess with **high confidence** that NATO TNT supply will remain a critical chokepoint through at least 2027.

## 4. Precursor Signals

### 4.1 Hexamine as Keystone Indicator

RDX is most commonly produced via the Bachmann process, which consumes approximately **2.5–3 kg of hexamine (hexamethylenetetramine) per kg of finished RDX**. Hexamine is therefore the keystone upstream signal: changes in hexamine availability, pricing, or trade flow precede changes in RDX output by roughly 1–3 months.

### 4.2 The Bakelite Tariff Paradox

**Bakelite LLC** — the sole US producer of hexamine — filed antidumping and countervailing duty (AD/CVD) petitions in October 2024 against imports from China, Germany, India, and Saudi Arabia (Admiralty A-1, USITC public docket).

The USITC issued a **preliminary affirmative determination in September 2025**, finding Chinese hexamine sold at less than fair value and subsidized.

This produces a genuine policy paradox:
- **Pro-protection logic:** A viable domestic hexamine producer is itself a national-security asset; killing Bakelite via dumping would create another single-point-of-failure.
- **Pro-import logic:** Constraining hexamine imports during an active RDX expansion programme could directly suppress US RDX output, with second-order effects on artillery, naval, and air munitions.

We assess with **moderate confidence** that the final USITC determination will impose duties but with structured exemptions or quotas for defense end-use — though we acknowledge this is essentially a forecast about an administrative-agency decision and carries meaningful tail risk in either direction.

### 4.3 Russian Hexamine

Russia ceased hexamine exports around 2022, redirecting domestic output to RDX production for the war in Ukraine. The **Metafrax Chemicals strike (February 16–17, 2026)** in Perm Krai is consequential precisely because Metafrax produces hexamine, methanol, and urea — all RDX precursor inputs. We assess with **moderate confidence** that the Metafrax strike measurably constrained Russian hexamine availability beginning in March–April 2026 — a constraint that compounds with subsequent strikes on Sverdlov itself.

### 4.4 What to Watch on Precursors

- **Hexamine spot pricing** in Chinese, Indian, and European markets (caveat: spot data is fragmentary).
- **Chinese hexamine export licensing** — any export-control move would be a strong signal of intentional supply leverage.
- **Cotton linter export flows from China to Europe** — analogous strategic dependency for NC.
- **Methanol and ammonia pricing** in Russian domestic markets — proxy for precursor distress post-strikes.

### 5.3 Cumulative Impact Assessment

**Sverdlov Plant (April 30, 2026)** is the most consequential single strike of the campaign. As Russia's sole dedicated RDX/HMX producer at approximately 8,000 t/yr nameplate capacity, any sustained throughput reduction propagates directly into Russian artillery shell production, anti-tank warhead manufacture, and air-delivered munitions filling.

Satellite-confirmed damage to production buildings has been reported (Admiralty B-2). However, we caveat the following:

- We **do not** have a reliable percent-of-capacity damage estimate. Public claims range from "limited" to "catastrophic."
- Russian industrial recovery from the 2023 Ust-Luga and other strikes has historically been **faster than initial Western estimates predicted**. Expectations of permanent destruction are usually wrong.
- Redundancy in less-publicized facilities or stockpiles may buffer near-term effects.

**Working estimate (low confidence):** Aggregate Russian military explosives production for Q2–Q3 2026 has been reduced by 15–40% relative to a no-strikes counterfactual. This range is deliberately wide; we will tighten it as imagery and trade-data analysis matures over Q3 2026.

### 5.4 Constraint on Russian Artillery Operations

The translation from RDX production loss to battlefield artillery expenditure is not 1-to-1. Russia maintains accumulated munitions stockpiles, can substitute North Korean and Iranian supply, and can reduce fill-quality (e.g., increased ammonium-nitrate-based formulations) at the cost of effectiveness. We therefore assess with **moderate confidence** that the strike campaign will manifest in measurable Russian artillery expenditure reductions on a **6–12 month lag**, not immediately — assuming the campaign is sustained and repairs are not completed faster than expected.

**What to look for:** First serial 155mm shell deliveries from the Hungarian plant; any commissioning delays reported.
**Why it matters:** Várpalota is the most visible piece of Rheinmetall's plan to reach 1.1M shells/year by 2027 (Admiralty B-2). Slippage here cascades into European 2M shell/year target.

### Signal 4 — Further Russian Explosives Plant Strikes
**What to look for:** Continued Ukrainian targeting of energetics, precursor, and propellant facilities. Particular attention to: any second strike on Sverdlov to prevent repair; strikes on Russian cotton linter / nitrocellulose infrastructure; strikes on rail nodes serving Dzerzhinsk industrial cluster.
**Why it matters:** Sustains pressure on Russian artillery throughput; conversely, a halt in strikes would suggest Ukrainian deep-strike capacity is itself constrained.

### Signal 5 — European NC Capacity Announcements
**What to look for:** New nitrocellulose plant commitments (size, location, financing); cotton linter sourcing diversification announcements (Brazil, Central Asia, US alternatives); BAE NC-free propellant industrial milestones.
**Why it matters:** Without NC capacity expansion, RDX expansion in Europe is downstream-stranded. This is the most underreported watch item in the European rearmament story.

## Analytic Confidence and Limitations

This is a **first validation report** on an analytical topic with significant open-source data gaps. Key limitations:

- **Market sizing figures vary widely across published sources** (Section 1.1) — we do not yet have a defensible single point estimate.
- **Russian damage assessments are imprecise.** Estimates of Sverdlov throughput loss carry low confidence and a wide range (15–40% aggregate Russian explosives impact).
- **Asia-Pacific producer capacities** (Hanwha, Solar Industries) are reported in qualitative terms; quantitative throughput data is limited.
- **Hexamine spot pricing data** is fragmentary, limiting the precision of precursor-signal monitoring.
- **The interaction between AD/CVD policy, defense procurement, and trade flows** is genuinely uncertain pending the USITC final determination.

We expect to materially tighten estimates in the Q3 2026 update as: (a) Holston FEM train commissioning data becomes available; (b) post-strike imagery of Russian facilities matures; (c) USITC issues final determination; (d) Q2 industrial output data from European producers is published.
