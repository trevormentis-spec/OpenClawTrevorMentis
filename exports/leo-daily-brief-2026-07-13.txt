═══ EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ═══

Two shifts. First, Amazon’s Kuiper has surfaced in the FCC database via Denali 20020, LLC—the first tangible licensing evidence that their ground network is moving from planning to deployment. Second, China’s Long March 6A and 8 are lofting unknown payloads from Taiyuan and Wenchang, likely the GuoWang or Hongyan LEO broadband prototypes. If confirmed, they represent a new demand vector for ground services outside the U.S.-centric market. The rest of the landscape holds steady: Starlink’s launch cadence remains aggressive (7 missions in the pipe), the tracked site register shows no critical risks, and hiring signals are tepid. Imagery is down, so we’re blind to construction progress at known gateway sites.

What it means: Kuiper’s move into licensing signals that ground station operators—especially those with assets in N. America and Europe—should brace for competitive lease negotiations and spectrum coordination tangles. The Chinese unknowns might be testing early, but if they follow the Starlink playbook, expect a scramble for global gateway locations within 12-18 months. Watch for ITU coordination filings (currently nil) and surges in ground engineer job postings as lead indicators of buildout pace. The market metrics continue to forecast a $5.33B LEO GSaaS sub-market by 2033, but the near-term action is in who secures the real estate and spectrum first.

═══ FCC LICENSES ═══

Notable new entry: Denali 20020, LLC—Amazon’s Kuiper licensing entity—appears in the licensee list. This is the first time we’ve seen them in this feed, confirming active earth station filings. The other 90+ records are renewals or amendments from incumbents (MCI, Inmarsat, Intelsat, AT&T, SES, Goonhilly, etc.). Starlink is conspicuously absent; they likely file under a different rubric or hold blanket authorizations. Mx1 Inc and MX1 Inc are duplicates, probable data artifact. No other disruptive licensees. Bottom line: Kuiper is real and moving through the regulatory pipeline; we need to map which sites they’re targeting and correlate with our risk register.

═══ CONSTELLATION SIGNALS ═══

Launches tell a mixed story. Starlink accounts for 7 of the 357 upcoming missions, maintaining a weekly rhythm. The next-five list includes three Starlink groups (17-47, 10-43, 17-43) with June nets that have almost certainly flown by now, so the feed lags. However, the cumulative effect is that Starlink’s on-orbit count is pushing past 9,400 toward the 12,000 mark, which demands additional ground gateways—especially for the polar shells. The real news is the two Chinese Long March launches: a 6A from Taiyuan and an 8 from Wenchang, both with “Unknown Payload.” Given the lack of transparency, these are almost certainly LEO broadband test satellites. GuoWang (the national constellation) or Hongyan (CASC’s project) are the likely candidates. Ground segment implications: China will need offshore gateways to achieve global coverage, creating opportunities at sites in friendly jurisdictions (e.g., BRI-aligned nations). We’ll track for ITU filings in the coming weeks.

However, if Kuiper and China accelerate, expect this number to spike within 6-9 months.
