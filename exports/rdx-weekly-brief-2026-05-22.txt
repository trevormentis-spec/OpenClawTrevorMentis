=== EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ===

The global market for RDX (Research Department Explosive) and C4 (Composition 4, a plastic explosive built around RDX) remains in a sustained expansion phase this week, with no major disruptive headlines but a clear pattern of broad-based hiring across every Western and allied producer tracked. The most important signal this cycle is the unanimous active hiring posture at six major producers on three continents. This is consistent with a structural supply build-out rather than a short-term surge, and it reinforces the view that Western governments are treating the energetic materials shortage as a multi-year industrial problem, not a temporary procurement gap. Trade remedy activity on hexamine, a key precursor chemical used to make RDX, continued to advance at the United States International Trade Commission, suggesting Washington is moving to wall off domestic precursor supply from foreign competition. Russian producer facilities remain offline or degraded following strikes earlier this spring, with no new strike events reported this week.

=== PRODUCER ACTIVITY ===

Holston Army Ammunition Plant (Tennessee, United States): The sole American producer of RDX and HMX (High Melting Explosive, a more powerful cousin of RDX) continues active hiring. The plant operates under a government-owned, contractor-operated arrangement with BAE Systems through 2033, and a multi-year modernization program is underway.

Eurenco Bergerac (France): Active hiring continues at the European RDX anchor facility, which restarted a previously idle production line to meet Ukrainian artillery demand.

Chemring Nobel (Norway): Active hiring at the Nordic producer of RDX, HMX, and PETN (Pentaerythritol Tetranitrate, used in detonators and boosters).

Rheinmetall Varpalota (Hungary): The new plant remains in commissioning. No new milestone announcements this week.

Nitro-Chem (Poland): Sole NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization) producer of TNT (Trinitrotoluene), continuing to execute its $310 million United States contract for 18,000 tons through 2029. No new public events this week.

Hanwha (South Korea) and Solar Industries (India): Both Asian producers are actively hiring, consistent with expansion of military and industrial explosives output.

Russian facilities: Sverdlov Plant (damaged April 30), Bryansk Chemical (struck May 2026), and JSC Promsintez (struck March 28) all remain in post-strike status with no new public information this week.

=== SIGNALS & INDICATORS ===

News flow was unusually quiet, with zero relevant articles surfacing through the GDELT (Global Database of Events, Language, and Tone) feed this cycle. This is a notable contrast to recent weeks and likely reflects a normal news lull rather than a change in underlying activity.

Hiring signals were the dominant data point. All six tracked Western and allied producers are in active hiring mode simultaneously, which has not been a constant condition over the past year.

Patent activity continues to cluster around insensitive munitions, meaning explosives engineered to resist accidental detonation from fire, shock, or shrapnel. Five recent filings touch on insensitive propellants, insensitive detonating substances, and pressable plastic-bound compositions. This confirms that the long-term technical migration away from legacy formulations toward insensitive variants remains the central research direction.

Trade remedy activity at the United States International Trade Commission continues to focus on hexamine, the precursor chemical for RDX, with Bakelite LLC pursuing antidumping duties. A separate proceeding on urea ammonium nitrate solutions, a precursor relevant to bulk industrial explosives, returned a finding of no injury from Russian and Trinidadian imports.

The Environmental Protection Agency Toxics Release Inventory, which we use as a rough proxy for production volumes at United States Army ammunition plants, returned no data this week for either Holston or Radford. This is a data availability issue, not a signal.

Conflict Armament Research, the organization that traces weapons recovered from battlefields, published no new RDX or C4 specific findings this week.
