=== EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ===

The most important development this week is the announcement of an 8.8 billion United States dollar contract awarded to BAE Systems to continue operating the Holston Army Ammunition Plant in Tennessee. Holston is the only facility in the United States that produces RDX (Research Department Explosive) and HMX (High Melting Explosive), the two most important military high explosives used in artillery shells, bombs, and C4 (Composition 4) plastic explosive. This contract locks in United States production capacity and modernization funding for years to come and signals that Washington views the RDX supply squeeze as a long-term structural problem rather than a short-term Ukraine-driven spike.

The second signal worth noting is that every major Western and allied producer we track is actively hiring. When six separate producers on three continents all expand headcount at the same time, it points to sustained, coordinated capacity growth rather than a one-off surge. No new Russian plant strikes were reported this week, suggesting a pause in the Ukrainian long-range campaign against Russian explosives infrastructure that ran from March through May 2026.

The bottom line: supply is expanding on the NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization) side, Russian supply remains degraded from earlier strikes, and precursor chemical trade cases in the United States could reshape input costs over the next quarter.

=== PRODUCER ACTIVITY ===

Holston Army Ammunition Plant, Tennessee, United States: BAE Systems won an 8.8 billion United States dollar contract extension to continue running the plant under the government-owned, contractor-operated model. Active hiring confirms modernization is moving from planning to execution.

Eurenco Bergerac, France: Active hiring continues. This is the European anchor for RDX and restarted a production line specifically to serve Ukrainian artillery demand.

Chemring Nobel, Norway: Active hiring. Produces RDX, HMX, and PETN (Pentaerythritol Tetranitrate).

Rheinmetall Varpalota, Hungary: Active hiring, consistent with commissioning of the new plant.

Hanwha, South Korea: Active hiring. Reinforces the pattern of Asia-Pacific capacity growth.

Solar Industries, India: Active hiring, consistent with the company's stated expansion into both mining explosives and military output.

Nitro-Chem, Poland: No new signals this week. The 310 million United States dollar United States contract for 18,000 tons of TNT (Trinitrotoluene) through 2029 remains the anchor commitment.

Russian producers (Sverdlov, Bryansk Chemical, JSC Promsintez): No new open-source intelligence events this week. All three were struck between March and May 2026 and remain in a damaged or degraded state based on prior reporting.

=== SIGNALS & INDICATORS ===

News flow: Light this week with only two relevant articles. The Holston contract dominates. A medical case report on seizures from C4 ingestion is a curiosity, not a market signal.

Hiring: Six of the major non-Russian producers are simultaneously hiring. This is the strongest signal in the data set this week and points to sustained capacity buildout.

Patents: The five patents pulled this week all cluster around insensitive munitions, which are explosives designed to resist accidental detonation from fire, shock, or fragment impact. This confirms the industry's continued migration away from traditional Composition B toward IMX (Insensitive Munitions Explosive) family formulations. This is a multi-year technology shift, not a weekly change.

Trade remedies: The United States International Trade Commission is running active antidumping cases on hexamine, a key precursor to RDX, driven by a petition from Bakelite LLC. Separately, urea ammonium nitrate cases involving Russia and Trinidad and Tobago produced a mixed outcome, with the commission finding no injury in one determination. Hexamine duties, if imposed, would raise RDX input costs for any United States producer sourcing imported hexamine.

Conflict Armament Research: No new publication this week, only the standard index pages appeared in the feed.

Environmental Protection Agency Toxics Release Inventory: Data for Holston and Radford remains unavailable, so we cannot use pollutant reporting as a production proxy this cycle.

Russian plant open-source intelligence: Quiet week. No confirmed new strikes.
