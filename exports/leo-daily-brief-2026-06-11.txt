EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Starlink continues to burn the candle at both ends. Three Falcon 9 missions across Group 17-47, 10-43, and 17-43 in a single five-day window, adding roughly 150–160 satellites to a constellation already north of 9,400. That pace is not new, but the shift toward the 17-series shells (Group 17-47, 17-43) suggests we’re entering the thick of Gen2 deployment at the 530–560 km altitude, where capacity density and ground station handoff pressure will spike. The immediate take: SpaceX is now running a cadence that requires roughly one new gateway every 3–4 weeks to keep up with per-satellite throughput, yet our site register shows Starlink at 38 ground sites globally—only a modest uptick from last quarter. The disconnect between launch tempo and ground infrastructure expansion is starting to look deliberate, possibly indicating SpaceX is squeezing more capacity out of existing gateways via optical inter-satellite links, or it’s about to drop a batch of new gateway filings. Either way, the GSaaS sub-market (projected $5.33B by 2033) and the broader ground segment ($82.7B by 2030) remain fundamentally dependent on Starlink’s next move.

What to watch: any sudden FCC gateway application wave, especially from SpaceX’s wholly-owned subsidiary (not yet listed in our feed, but that list may be filtered). Also, the two Chinese Long March launches with unknown payloads—if either is a LEO broadband prototype (GalaxySpace, Geespace, or a military comsat), it could trigger a new round of ITU filings and ground station demand in Asia-Pacific. For now, the market is eerily quiet on the regulatory front. Jobs signals are near-zero; ITU feed empty; imagery feed is down. We’re flying partially blind.

═══ CONSTELLATION SIGNALS ═══

Seven Starlink launches are in the total upcoming manifest (357 total), with three of the next five missions being Starlink. That’s aggressive even by their standards. The specific groups: 17-47 and 17-43 are Gen2 shells; 10-43 could be a replenishment for the older 540 km shell. This tempo means SpaceX will add ~120 satellites per week, pushing the constellation to ~9,600 by month-end. At current average throughput per satellite (~20 Gbps Ka/Ku), that’s roughly 2.4 Tbps of new space-side capacity weekly. Ground gateways need to match that with at least 10–12 new 8-meter antennas per week globally, assuming 4x frequency reuse and 80% utilization. Our site register shows Starlink at 38 sites total, with only a few in build. If that number doesn’t jump to 45+ by Q3, we’ll know optical ISLs are bearing the load. That’s a strategic signal: SpaceX is prioritizing space-side mesh over ground build-out to maintain cash flow, which could depress GSaaS demand in the short term but eventually require a massive gateway catch-up when user terminals scale to 14M+ (projected 2030). Amazon’s Kuiper remains at 5 sites—no acceleration visible. OneWeb (10 sites) and Telesat (3 sites) are flatlining. The LEO ground game is a one-horse race right now.
