=== EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ===

The dominant signal this week is the confirmation of an 8.8 billion United States dollar contract award to BAE Systems to continue operating the Holston Army Ammunition Plant in Tennessee. Holston is the only facility in the United States that produces RDX (Research Department Explosive) and HMX (High Melting Explosive), the two most important military high explosives used in artillery shells, warheads, and Composition 4 (C4) plastic explosive. The contract runs alongside the existing Government-Owned Contractor-Operated arrangement that extends through 2033 and locks in modernization funding.

The practical meaning is straightforward. Washington has now committed multi-year, multi-billion dollar money to expanding domestic explosive fill capacity, ending any remaining doubt about whether the Ukraine-driven surge in artillery demand is being treated as a temporary spike or a structural shift. It is being treated as structural.

Second-order signal: every major Western and allied producer we track is actively hiring. That has not been true in any prior week this year. Combined with continued hexamine (an RDX precursor chemical) antidumping activity at the United States International Trade Commission, the picture is one of Western producers scaling up while trying to insulate their input chemistry from Chinese supply.

No new Russian plant strikes were reported this cycle, the first quiet week since the spring 2026 campaign against Sverdlov, Bryansk Chemical, and JSC Promsintez.

=== SIGNALS AND INDICATORS ===

News flow: Light, only two relevant items. One is the BAE Systems contract. The other is a medical case report on seizures caused by voluntary ingestion of C4, useful only as a reminder that RDX toxicity remains a workforce and public health issue at production sites.

Hiring: Six of the major Western and allied producers we track are simultaneously in active hiring mode. This is the clearest leading indicator of capacity expansion we have, and it is uniformly positive.

Patents: Five patent items surfaced this cycle, all clustered around insensitive munitions, a category of explosive that resists accidental detonation from fire, shock, or shrapnel. Insensitive formulations typically substitute or bind RDX and HMX in new ways. This confirms the ongoing migration of Western military demand away from legacy Composition B toward insensitive fills, which changes the RDX and HMX volume mix but not the total tonnage.

Trade remedies: Three separate United States International Trade Commission items on hexamine antidumping, including a Bakelite LLC petition and final affirmative determinations. Hexamine is the key feedstock for RDX synthesis. Duties on imported hexamine will raise input costs for United States producers in the near term but are intended to rebuild a domestic hexamine supply base. Two additional items cover urea ammonium nitrate solution from Russia and Trinidad and Tobago, relevant to the broader energetic materials precursor chain.

Conflict Armament Research: Standard publication surfacing, no new specific tracing report this cycle relevant to RDX or C4 diversion.

Environmental Protection Agency Toxics Release Inventory: Holston and Radford data remain unavailable, limiting our ability to independently estimate output.

Russian plant open-source intelligence: No events this week.
