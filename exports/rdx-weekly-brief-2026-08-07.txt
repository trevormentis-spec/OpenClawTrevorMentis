=== EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ===

The dominant story this week is the United States government's award of an $8.8 billion contract to BAE Systems to continue operating the Holston Army Ammunition Plant in Tennessee, the only American producer of Research Department Explosive (RDX) and its more powerful cousin High Melting Explosive (HMX). This single announcement locks in the long-term backbone of the Western explosives supply chain and signals that Washington expects sustained high demand for military-grade explosives well into the next decade.

Beyond that headline, the picture across the market is one of coordinated expansion. Every major non-Russian producer we track is actively hiring, from France to Norway to Hungary to South Korea to India. Trade remedy actions on precursor chemicals, specifically hexamine and ammonium nitrate, continue to move through the United States International Trade Commission (USITC), which will shape input costs for months to come. No new Russian plant strikes were reported this week, a pause after several months of Ukrainian long-range attacks on Russian production sites.

The bottom line: Western capacity is being rebuilt at scale, Russian capacity is degraded but not this week further damaged, and the precursor chemical supply chain is the quiet variable that could still tighten prices.

=== PRODUCER ACTIVITY ===

Holston Army Ammunition Plant, Tennessee. The $8.8 billion award to BAE Systems, a British defense contractor operating the plant under a government-owned, contractor-operated (GOCO) arrangement, extends operations and funds ongoing modernization. Active hiring continues. This is the most significant single event of the week.

Eurenco Bergerac, France. The company remains in active hiring mode. The Bergerac line, restarted specifically to feed Ukrainian artillery demand, is now a fixed anchor of European supply.

Chemring Nobel, Norway. Active hiring for RDX, HMX, and pentaerythritol tetranitrate (PETN) production lines.

Rheinmetall Varpalota, Hungary. The new German-owned plant in Hungary is in commissioning phase. Active hiring aligns with expected ramp-up.

Hanwha, South Korea. Active hiring. South Korea has become a critical alternative supplier to European customers seeking to diversify away from single-source risk.

Solar Industries, India. Active hiring supports announced expansion in both industrial mining explosives and military output.

Nitro-Chem, Poland. No new events this week. The company remains the sole North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) producer of trinitrotoluene (TNT) and is delivering under its $310 million United States contract for 18,000 tons through 2029.

Russian facilities. No new strike events reported this cycle. Sverdlov Plant, Bryansk Chemical, and JSC Promsintez remain in various states of damage from strikes earlier in 2026.

=== SIGNALS AND INDICATORS ===

News flow. Two relevant articles surfaced. The BAE contract is the market-moving item. A separate medical case report on seizures following intentional ingestion of C-4, a plastic explosive whose main ingredient is RDX, is noise from a market perspective.

Hiring. Six of the major Western and allied producers are actively hiring. This is the clearest available leading indicator of expansion, and it points in one direction: up.

Patents. Five recent patents cluster around insensitive munitions, meaning explosives designed to resist accidental detonation from fire, shock, or nearby blasts. The Western industry is investing research money in safer formulations even as it scales up traditional RDX and HMX output. This is a multi-year technology shift, not a near-term price driver.

Trade remedies. The USITC is active on two precursor fronts. Bakelite LLC is pursuing antidumping duties on hexamine, a key raw material for RDX. Separately, the USITC issued a mixed decision on urea ammonium nitrate solution imports from Russia and Trinidad and Tobago, finding no injury in that specific case but continuing related investigations. Precursor pricing remains a variable to monitor.

Conflict Armament Research. The independent munitions-tracing organization based in London continues publishing, but nothing new this week directly ties to RDX or C-4 supply chains.

Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Toxics Release Inventory data. Reporting for Holston and Radford Army Ammunition Plants remains unavailable this cycle, limiting our ability to use pollutant releases as a production proxy.

Russian open-source intelligence. Quiet week. No confirmed new strikes or accidents at Russian production sites.

=== WHAT TO WATCH ===

1. Hexamine antidumping ruling progression at the USITC. A final affirmative determination would raise the cost of a core RDX precursor for United States buyers. Likely (55-70 percent) that duties tighten hexamine costs modestly within 30 days.

2. Rheinmetall Varpalota commissioning milestones in Hungary. First output announcements would materially expand European supply. Likely (55-70 percent) that a public commissioning or first-production statement appears within 30 days.

3. Further Ukrainian strikes on Russian explosives or precursor facilities. After a quiet week, the pattern from March through May 2026 suggests periodic resumption. Highly likely (75-85 percent) that at least one additional strike attempt on a Russian chemical or munitions site occurs within 30 days.

4. Additional United States or European contract awards downstream of the BAE Holston deal. Large prime contracts typically trigger subcontractor announcements within weeks. Likely (55-70 percent) that at least one supporting subcontract on Holston modernization becomes public within 30 days.

5. European nitrocellulose gap announcements. With European output at 4,500 to 10,000 tons per year against a 20,000 ton target, new investment or joint-venture news is overdue. Likely (55-70 percent) that a European government or company announces new nitrocellulose capacity within 30 days.
