═══ CONSTELLATION SIGNALS ═══

Launch activity remains dominated by Starlink: three Falcon 9 missions (Groups 17-47, 10-43, 17-43) within the next five launch windows, part of a total of seven Starlink launches in the upcoming 357-launch queue. Assuming 22-23 satellites per launch, that’s roughly 150-160 new on-orbit assets per month, pushing the constellation toward the 10,000-satellite mark by early Q3 2026. Each new satellite demands backend ground capacity—scale that to 12,000 or beyond, and the gateway network faces a compound stress test. Starlink owns 38 ground stations in our register, but that’s just the known count; expect SpaceX to press for additional sites, especially in under-served Asia-Pacific and African markets.

More intriguing: two Chinese Long March launches with “Unknown Payload” on 4 and 5 June. While deliberately opaque, the context points to early deployments of China’s Guowang LEO broadband constellation (or a test bed like Spacesail). If Guowang targets 13,000 satellites, the ground infrastructure requirements—both domestic and along BRI corridors—will become a strategic play. This could mean a burst of ITU coordination filings, earth station licenses across Central Asia, Middle East, and Africa, and a scramble for partnership deals. The ground market isn’t pricing this in yet; the first clear signal will be ITU filings or public disclosure from Chinese entities.
