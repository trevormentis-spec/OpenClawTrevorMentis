=== EXECUTIVE SUMMARY ===

The most important development this week is the confirmation of an 8.8 billion United States dollar contract awarded to BAE Systems to continue operating the Holston Army Ammunition Plant in Tennessee. Holston is the only facility in the United States that produces RDX (Research Department Explosive) and HMX (High Melting Explosive), the two core military explosive fillers used in artillery shells, missile warheads, and C4 (Composition 4) plastic explosive. This contract locks in operator continuity and funds ongoing modernization, and it signals that Washington now treats domestic explosive fill capacity as a long-term strategic requirement rather than a short-term surge need.

The broader picture is one of coordinated Western capacity expansion running alongside continued degradation of Russian production. Hiring is active at six of the major Western and allied producers at once, which is an unusual concentration. Meanwhile, no new Russian plant events surfaced this week, but the three Russian facilities struck earlier in 2026 remain the backdrop against which allied expansion is being planned.

What it means: the market is transitioning from an emergency surge posture, driven by Ukraine artillery consumption, into a durable multi-year buildout. Buyers should expect firmer pricing, longer lead times on RDX and HMX, and continued political attention to precursor chemicals such as hexamine and ammonium nitrate.

=== PRODUCER ACTIVITY ===

Holston Army Ammunition Plant, Tennessee, United States: The 8.8 billion dollar Government-Owned, Contractor-Operated award to BAE Systems extends the operator relationship through 2033 and funds modernization already underway. Hiring is active. This is the single largest signal of the week.

Eurenco Bergerac, France: Continues active hiring. The Bergerac line, restarted specifically to feed Ukraine-related artillery demand, remains the European RDX anchor.

Chemring Nobel, Norway: Active hiring. Chemring Nobel produces RDX, HMX, and PETN (Pentaerythritol Tetranitrate) and remains a key non-United States NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization) supplier.

Rheinmetall Varpalota, Hungary: Active hiring, consistent with the new plant moving through commissioning. This facility is the most important new Western capacity coming online in the next 18 months.

Hanwha, South Korea: Active hiring. Hanwha is the largest Asian military explosives producer and an increasingly important backfill supplier to European buyers.

Solar Industries, India: Active hiring, tied to expansion of both industrial and military output.

Nitro-Chem, Poland: No new events this week. The 310 million dollar United States contract for 18,000 tons of TNT (Trinitrotoluene) through 2029 remains in force. Nitro-Chem is the only NATO TNT producer.

Russian producers: No new open-source events this week involving Sverdlov Plant, Bryansk Chemical, or JSC Promsintez. All three were struck earlier in 2026 and no public evidence of full restoration has emerged.

=== SIGNALS & INDICATORS ===

News flow: Two relevant news items this cycle. The Holston contract dominates. A medical case report on cyclonite (another name for RDX) ingestion is not market-relevant but confirms continued academic attention to RDX toxicology.

Hiring: Six of the major Western and allied producers are hiring simultaneously. This is the clearest leading indicator of capacity expansion and is consistent with a multi-year buildout rather than a temporary surge.

Patents: Five patents in the sample cluster around insensitive munitions and insensitive high explosives. Insensitive munitions are warheads and propellants designed not to detonate accidentally from fire, shock, or nearby explosions. The patent direction confirms an ongoing migration away from traditional Composition B and toward RDX and HMX based insensitive formulations, which supports demand for the higher-value explosive grades.

Trade remedies: The United States International Trade Commission has active antidumping actions on hexamine, a direct precursor to RDX, driven by a petition from Bakelite LLC. Separately, the Commission voted to continue investigations on Urea Ammonium Nitrate Solution from Russia and Trinidad and Tobago, though a preliminary finding indicated no injury from those specific imports. The hexamine action is the more consequential of the two for RDX economics, because domestic hexamine pricing feeds directly into Holston input costs.

Conflict Armament Research: Standard publication traffic, no specific new tracing findings this week that alter the market picture.

Environmental Protection Agency Toxics Release Inventory: Data for Holston and Radford Army Ammunition Plants remains unavailable this cycle, so the usual production proxy is dark. This is a recurring gap and analysts should not read anything into the absence.

Russian open-source events: None this week.

=== WHAT TO WATCH ===

1. Hexamine antidumping ruling: Watch for the next procedural step from the United States International Trade Commission on the Bakelite LLC hexamine petition. It is likely (55 to 70 percent) that final duties will be imposed within the next 30 to 90 days, which would raise the input cost of RDX production at Holston.

2. Rheinmetall Varpalota commissioning milestones: Watch for a public announcement of first production or first delivery from the Hungarian plant. It is likely (55 to 70 percent) that a milestone announcement occurs within 30 days given the hiring pace.

3. Follow-on strikes or restoration reporting on Russian producers: Watch for open-source imagery or statements concerning Sverdlov, Bryansk Chemical, or JSC Promsintez. It is highly likely (75 to 85 percent) that at least one of these three sites will generate a public signal, either damage assessment or restoration claim, within 30 days.

4. European nitrocellulose gap: Watch for any European Union or member state funding announcement aimed at closing the gap between current 4,500 to 10,000 tons per year output and the 20,000 tons per year target. Nitrocellulose is not RDX but it is the choke point for finished artillery propellant, and a fresh funding move is likely (55 to 70 percent) in the next 30 days.

5. Holston modernization sub-awards: Watch for BAE Systems sub-contract announcements flowing from the new 8.8 billion dollar prime contract. It is highly likely (75 to 85 percent) that at least one significant sub-award to a construction, process equipment, or automation vendor becomes public within 30 days.
