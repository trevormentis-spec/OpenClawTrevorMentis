## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Judgment One: The Iran nuclear deal complex exhibits the largest cross-venue divergence on the board, and it is structural rather than informational.** Polymarket's "Iran nuclear deal before 2027" prints 64¢ against Kalshi's year-end 2026 contract at 50.5¢, a 13.5 percentage point gap on what superficially appears to be the same question. The explanation is almost entirely resolution criteria: Polymarket resolves on credible reporting of a framework agreement, while Kalshi's USAIRAN series demands a formal, signed nuclear accord. This is not an arbitrage; it is a definitional spread that should be traded as a relative-value structure rather than a convergence bet.

**Judgment Two: Kalshi's own internal term structure on Iran is self-consistent and implies a roughly linear monthly hazard rate of ~3.2% with a modest acceleration into 2027.** The progression from June (10.5¢) to August (32.5¢) to YE26 (50.5¢) to Jan28 (75.5¢) fits a coherent Poisson-like arrival process. The shortest-dated June contract is the most mispriced leg relative to base rates of diplomatic timelines, and the August contract is the richest leg relative to the implied forward.

**Judgment Three: The Kalshi "Trump personally negotiates Iran deal" contract at 9% versus the generic deal contract at 50.5% creates a 41.5pp wedge that is the cleanest signal on the board.** The market is explicitly pricing that any deal happens through Witkoff, State, or back-channel intermediaries rather than direct Trump involvement. This wedge is too wide given Trump's documented preference for personal credit-claiming on signature foreign policy wins, and it offers a directional long on the "personally" leg as a cheap optionality structure.

## 3. KEY DIVERGENCES

### Divergence 1: Iran Deal — Polymarket "Before 2027" 64¢ vs Kalshi YE26 50.5¢

The headline 13.5pp gap is the most-watched divergence in macro prediction markets right now, but it is largely a definitional artifact. Polymarket's contract resolves YES on credible reporting of a framework or interim agreement; Kalshi requires a formal, named, signed nuclear deal posted to a verifiable government source. Historically, the gap between "framework reported" and "formal deal signed" in Iran negotiations has been months to years — the JCPOA framework was announced April 2015 and the final deal signed July 2015, a three-month lag, but informal frameworks in 2003-2005 and 2012-2013 never converted to formal deals at all.

Trade implication: the 13.5pp gap is approximately fair value for the framework-to-formal conversion probability conditional on framework occurring. A pure convergence trade (long Kalshi, short Polymarket) is not attractive; the structural spread is the trade if you have a view on conversion mechanics.

### Divergence 2: Iran Deal — Polymarket "May 31" 16.5¢ vs Kalshi June 10.5¢

This 6pp gap is more interesting because the resolution windows are nearly identical and both contracts effectively measure the same near-term event. Polymarket's May 31 contract is 16.5¢ and Kalshi's June contract is 10.5¢, meaning Polymarket is pricing an event roughly one month sooner at a 6pp premium. Adjusting for the shorter window, Polymarket's implied daily hazard rate is materially higher than Kalshi's.

The likely explanation is again resolution-criteria spread (Polymarket's looser framework definition catches more positive-flavored news events), combined with Polymarket's structurally higher retail flow which tends to overweight headline-driven news cycles. The cleaner trade here is short Polymarket May 31 and long Kalshi June as a paired structure, because the Polymarket leg has a defined hard expiry that forces resolution and the Kalshi leg provides protection against an actual near-term breakthrough.

### Divergence 3: Trump Personally vs Generic Iran Deal — 9% vs 50.5%

The 41.5pp wedge between "Trump personally negotiates" (9%) and "deal happens" (50.5%) implies the market assigns roughly an 18% conditional probability that, if a deal occurs, Trump is the personal negotiator of record. This is too low given the administration's documented pattern of Trump claiming personal credit on Abraham Accords-style announcements and the structural reality that any signed Iran deal would be a defining foreign policy moment that Trump would not delegate cleanly to Witkoff or Rubio for the photo opportunity.

The counterargument is that "personally negotiates" likely has a strict resolution criterion requiring Trump to be the named lead negotiator throughout the process, not just at the signing ceremony — which is genuinely unlikely given Witkoff's operational role. Even haircutting for this, 9% feels light; 15-20% is a more defensible fair value, making the "personally" leg a 6-11pp underpriced lottery ticket.

## 4. TERM STRUCTURE: IRAN DEAL HAZARD RATES

The Kalshi USAIRAN curve provides four clean term-structure observations that can be fit to an implied hazard rate function. Treating each contract as P(deal by date T) and assuming a constant monthly hazard rate λ, the survival function is S(t) = (1-λ)^t and the cumulative deal probability is 1 - (1-λ)^t.

**Anchor points (months from now, approximate):**
- June: t≈1, P = 10.5%
- August: t≈3, P = 32.5%
- YE26 (Dec 2026): t≈19, P = 50.5%
- Jan28: t≈32, P = 75.5%

**Implied monthly hazard rates from each anchor:**

For June (t=1): λ = 1 - (1-0.105)^(1/1) = 10.5% monthly. This is the front-month implied hazard.

For August (t=3): λ = 1 - (1-0.325)^(1/3) = 1 - 0.675^0.333 = 1 - 0.877 = 12.3% monthly. The market is pricing an *acceleration* between June and August, which is consistent with a known near-term negotiation calendar (Oman/Rome talk cadence).

For YE26 (t=19): λ = 1 - (1-0.505)^(1/19) = 1 - 0.495^0.0526 = 1 - 0.9637 = 3.63% monthly. This is dramatically lower than the front-month rate, implying the market expects negotiation momentum to decelerate sharply after the summer window.

For Jan28 (t=32): λ = 1 - (1-0.755)^(1/32) = 1 - 0.245^0.03125 = 1 - 0.9572 = 4.28% monthly. The long-dated hazard rises modestly above the YE26-implied rate, suggesting the market prices a small "second wind" probability through 2027.

**Forward hazard rates (between anchor points):**

June → August (2-month forward): P(deal in window | no deal by June) = (0.325 - 0.105)/(1 - 0.105) = 0.220/0.895 = 24.6%, implying a forward monthly hazard of 1 - (1-0.246)^0.5 = 13.2%. The August leg is the richest leg on the curve.

August → YE26 (16-month forward): P(deal in window | no deal by August) = (0.505 - 0.325)/(1 - 0.325) = 0.180/0.675 = 26.7%, implying a forward monthly hazard of 1 - (1-0.267)^(1/16) = 1.93%. This is the cheapest leg of the curve — the market is pricing very little incremental probability across the entire September 2025 to December 2026 window.

YE26 → Jan28 (13-month forward): P(deal in window | no deal by YE26) = (0.755 - 0.505)/(1 - 0.505) = 0.250/0.495 = 50.5%, implying a forward monthly hazard of 5.36%. Long-dated hazard reaccelerates.

**Interpretation:** The Kalshi curve has a distinctive "U-shape" in forward hazard rates: high in the immediate 1-3 month window (12-13% monthly), collapsing to <2% across the broad mid-curve, then rising back to ~5% in the long-dated 2027 window. This is the signature of a market pricing a binary "deal happens in the active negotiating window or it falls into a multi-year cold storage from which it eventually emerges."

**Where the curve is dislocated:** The August → YE26 forward at 1.93% monthly is materially below any reasonable base rate for active US-Iran negotiations, which historically have produced framework events at roughly 5-8% monthly hazard during live windows. If you believe negotiations remain alive past August, the YE26 contract is cheap relative to the August contract. The relative-value trade is short Kalshi August at 32.5¢ and long Kalshi YE26 at 50.5¢ in a roughly 1:1.5 ratio to neutralize the directional component.
