═══ POWER & GRID ═══
PJM’s interconnection queue has swollen to 122 GW, with a 4–6 year wait for new large loads. This is the driver behind the MODERATE risk rating for Northern Virginia’s Ashburn cluster—the largest globally—and explains why six of the eight highest‑risk facilities are Equinix campuses in grid‑strained US metros (Ashburn, Dallas, Chicago, San Jose, LA, Newark, New York). Meanwhile, AWS’s 1,200 MW permit filing for us‑east‑3 in Ohio will further stress a PJM sub‑market already contending with retiring baseload generation. The interconnection bottleneck is now a binding constraint on hyperscale growth; it is actively shaping site selection, pushing operators toward markets with pre‑existing capacity, even if that means higher energy costs or weaker connectivity.

Transformer lead times 18–24 months (worsening) are the most acute supply‑side pain point. Switchgear at 12–18 months and generators at 8–12 months compound the issue. No immediate price relief expected; some developers are reportedly paying 2–3x premiums for expedited delivery slots.
